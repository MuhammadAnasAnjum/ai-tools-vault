# Gleap: In-App Bug Reports With an AI Agent That Writes the Fix

Most feedback tools stop at collecting the complaint. Gleap's bet in 2026 is that the interesting work starts after that: read the session replay, read the console logs, read the codebase, then open a pull request with a proposed fix.

That is a genuinely ambitious claim, and it is what separates Gleap from the crowded field of in-app feedback widgets. It also arrives with a pricing structure that needs reading carefully.

## What is Gleap?

Gleap is a customer feedback and support platform that lives inside your product. A single SDK adds bug reporting with screenshots and annotations, session replay, a support inbox, feature request voting, a public roadmap, surveys, and a help centre.

The 2026 repositioning centres on **Kai**, its AI agent layer. **Kai Resolve** triages incoming tickets — deduplicating semantically similar reports, classifying severity, and routing to the right person. **Kai Code** goes further: it reads the session replay, console output, and your codebase, plans a fix, and opens a pull request for a developer to review.

The company is independent and still shipping. Its pricing was revised during 2026, and the tier structure changed enough that older comparison articles describe plans that no longer exist.

## Key Features

- **One SDK, every platform** — Web, iOS, Android, React Native, Flutter, and desktop from a single integration.
- **Rich bug capture** — annotated screenshots, session replay, console logs, and device context attached automatically, so you stop asking users for reproduction steps.
- **Kai Resolve** — semantic deduplication, severity classification, and automatic routing of incoming reports.
- **Kai Code** — reads replays, logs, and your repository, then proposes a fix as a pull request.
- **Multi-channel support inbox** — WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger, Telegram, and email alongside in-app chat.
- **Feature voting and public roadmap** — turn requests into a prioritised, visible backlog.
- **AI reply drafting and thread summaries** on Team and higher tiers.
- **API, custom domain, and a HubSpot importer** on higher plans.

## Pros and Cons

**The genuine strengths**

The capture quality is the everyday win. Getting a report with the session replay, console errors, and device details already attached eliminates the back-and-forth that consumes most triage time. That alone justifies a tool like this for many product teams.

Kai Code is the differentiator worth testing. Even when its proposed fix needs rework, having a pull request that identifies the relevant code path saves real investigation time on straightforward bugs. Consolidation is the other argument: bug capture, support inbox, feature voting, roadmap, and help centre in one subscription replaces two or three separate tools, and the multi-channel inbox means you are not running a separate support product alongside it.

**The real friction**

There is no free plan — a trial only, reportedly fourteen days. A small number of listings claim a free single-project tier; treat those as unreliable.

More importantly, **AI tokens bill separately on top of every plan**, so the sticker price is not your bill. A team leaning hard on Kai Resolve and Kai Code should expect meaningful usage charges above the subscription, and Gleap does not make that easy to forecast in advance. This is the single thing to model before committing.

The entry tier is also tight: one seat, one project, and most AI features locked, which makes it more of a proving ground than a working plan for a real team. API access sits behind higher tiers too, so integration work may force an upgrade you did not budget for.

Pricing is in flux and worth checking directly. The newer tier set is reported as Starter around $49/month annually, Team around $149, Pro around $299, and Enterprise from roughly $999. Older listings still show a Hobby plan near $39 and different Enterprise figures. Verify on Gleap's own pricing page before purchasing.

## How to Use Gleap

1. **Start the trial and install the SDK** in a staging build first. Integration is quick, and testing there avoids surprising real users with a new widget.
2. **Configure what gets captured** before going live. Session replay and console logs are the valuable part, so confirm they are enabled and that you have handled any sensitive data appropriately.
3. **Route reports to real owners.** An unrouted inbox becomes a graveyard within a fortnight. Set up ownership rules on day one.
4. **Let Kai Resolve triage for a week before trusting it.** Review its severity calls and deduplication against your own judgement, then tighten the rules where it got things wrong.
5. **Test Kai Code on a small, well-understood bug first.** Judge whether the pull requests are useful on something where you already know the right answer.
6. **Open the feature voting board to users** once the bug pipeline is working. Doing both at once produces noise you cannot process.
7. **Track your token spend weekly** during the trial so you know your true monthly cost before the plan renews.

### FAQs

**Does Gleap have a free plan?**
No. Gleap offers a trial, reportedly fourteen days, but no permanent free tier. Some third-party listings mention a free single-project plan; those appear unreliable and are not supported by the vendor's own materials. Budget for a paid plan from the start.

**What does Gleap actually cost per month?**
The subscription is only part of it, because **AI token usage is billed on top of every tier**. Reported subscription tiers are roughly $49/month for Starter annually, $149 for Team, $299 for Pro, and from about $999 for Enterprise, though pricing changed during 2026 and older figures circulate widely. Model your expected AI usage separately, then verify current tiers on Gleap's pricing page.

**Can Kai Code really fix bugs on its own?**
It proposes fixes as pull requests by reading session replay, console logs, and your codebase. Treat those as a well-researched starting point requiring developer review, not as merge-ready work. The realistic value is that it narrows down where the problem lives, which is often the slowest part of debugging.

**How does Gleap compare to Intercom or Sentry?**
It overlaps with both without fully replacing either. Sentry is stronger at error monitoring at scale; Intercom is stronger as a mature support and messaging platform. Gleap's argument is consolidation — in-app bug capture, support inbox, roadmap, and help centre for one bill, aimed at small and mid-sized product teams rather than large support organisations.
