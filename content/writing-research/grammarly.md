# Grammarly: Still the Best Writing Checker, Now Owned by a Company Called Superhuman

Grammarly is the most widely used writing assistant in the world, and it is also the tool most often described inaccurately, because the company behind it changed its name and most published reviews have not caught up.

Here is the clarification that resolves the confusion up front: **the company is now called Superhuman. The writing product is still called Grammarly.** If you subscribe today, you subscribe to Grammarly, and the app on your machine is Grammarly.

## What is Grammarly?

Grammarly checks and improves writing wherever you type. It began as a grammar and spelling checker, added tone and clarity suggestions, and now includes generative features that draft, rewrite, shorten and re-tone text on request.

Its real advantage has never been the underlying intelligence. It is distribution. Grammarly runs as a browser extension, a Word add-in, a desktop app, a mobile keyboard and an SDK, so it works inside email, documents, chat tools and forms without you moving text anywhere. A general chatbot can rewrite a paragraph better than Grammarly in isolation; Grammarly catches the paragraph you were never going to paste into a chatbot.

On **29 October 2025 the parent company rebranded to Superhuman**, taking the name of the email client it had acquired that July for a reported $825 million. It also owns Coda, acquired in 2024 and renamed Superhuman Docs in July 2026. The suite now spans four products: Grammarly for writing, Superhuman Mail, Superhuman Docs and Superhuman Go, a newer assistant that connects to tools like Gmail, Calendar, Drive and Jira.

Worth knowing for context on the company's stability: in May 2025 it raised **$1 billion in non-dilutive financing** from General Catalyst. Because that financing was non-dilutive, it set no new valuation, so the frequently repeated $13 billion figure dates from 2021 and should not be treated as current.

## Key Features

- **Grammar, spelling and punctuation checking** — still the most accurate mainstream implementation, and the reason most people are here.
- **Clarity and conciseness suggestions** that cut padding rather than just fixing errors.
- **Tone detection and re-toning**, useful for catching an email that reads colder than you intended.
- **Generative AI assistance** — drafting, rewriting, summarising and shortening, included in the plans rather than sold separately.
- **Style guides and brand tones** on paid plans, so teams write consistently.
- **Plagiarism detection** against web and academic sources.
- **AI detector**, with the reliability caveats every detector deserves.
- **Extremely broad integration** — browser extension, Microsoft Word, desktop apps, mobile keyboards, Google Docs.

## Pros and Cons

**What it gets right**

Accuracy on mechanical correctness is the core strength, and it is unglamorous but valuable. Grammarly catches subject-verb disagreement, comma splices and misused homophones with fewer false alarms than the checkers built into word processors. For anyone writing professionally in a second language, or anyone whose typos cost them credibility, that alone justifies the free tier.

The integration breadth genuinely matters. Grammarly improves the writing you do without thinking about it — Slack messages, support replies, LinkedIn posts — which is where most people's writing quality actually gets judged. Team features like shared style guides solve a real organisational problem that no general chatbot addresses.

**What to weigh carefully**

The billing presentation is the most common source of frustration. Pro is advertised at **$12 per member per month, but that is the annualised rate**, billed as roughly $144 per seat up front. The genuine month-to-month price is around $30. Reading "$12/month" and being charged $144 is a predictable shock, and it is the single most important thing to understand before subscribing.

Refunds compound it. Grammarly's stated policy is to issue refunds only where required by law, so an unwanted annual renewal is not straightforwardly reversible. Trustpilot ratings sit near 1.7 out of 5 across thousands of reviews, with hundreds of complaints logged with the Better Business Bureau over roughly three years, and the dominant theme is refunds rather than cancellation — cancelling is easy, undoing a charge is not.

The company also does not currently disclose which underlying language models power its generative features. Note that GPT, Claude and Gemini appear in Grammarly's documentation as *detection targets* for its AI detector, not as its engines; several reviews misread this as model disclosure.

**The lawsuit worth knowing about**

In March 2026 a class action was filed in federal court in Manhattan against Superhuman Platform, with journalist and author Julia Angwin as lead plaintiff. It concerns **Expert Review**, a feature launched in August 2025 that offered feedback written in the style of named real writers and academics without their consent. Grammarly withdrew the feature, called the claims without merit, and its chief executive publicly apologised and acknowledged the criticism had merit.

The important scoping point, since this gets conflated: the case is about **the identities of the named experts**, not about Grammarly training on customer documents. Those are separate concerns and the lawsuit does not speak to the second.

## How to Use Grammarly

1. **Install the free version first** and use it for two weeks. For many people the free tier is sufficient, and you cannot tell whether you need Pro until you see what free catches.
2. **Install it where you actually write.** The browser extension covers most people; add the Word add-in if you draft in Word.
3. **Set your writing goals** — audience, formality, intent. Suggestions change meaningfully based on these, and most users never touch them.
4. **Treat suggestions as proposals, not corrections.** Grammarly is confident about style choices that are genuinely matters of preference, and accepting everything flattens your voice.
5. **Check the billing screen carefully before paying.** Confirm whether you are buying an annual term or a monthly one, and what the total charge today will be.
6. **Turn off auto-renewal immediately after subscribing** if you only want a fixed period, given the refund policy.
7. **Do not rely on the AI detector to judge anyone's writing**, including your own. No detector on the market is reliable enough for that.

### FAQs

**Is Grammarly now called Superhuman?**
The company is; the product is not. Superhuman Platform, Inc. became the parent company name in October 2025, but the writing assistant is still sold and branded as Grammarly. Superhuman Mail, Superhuman Docs and Superhuman Go are separate products in the same suite.

**What happened to GrammarlyGO?**
The GrammarlyGO branding was retired and its generative capabilities were folded into Grammarly's general AI assistant, where drafting, rewriting and re-toning now live at no additional cost. If a review still describes GrammarlyGO as a separate add-on, it is out of date.

**How much does Grammarly Pro cost?**
Pro is advertised at around $12 per member per month, but that figure is billed annually as a single charge of roughly $144 per seat. Month-to-month is closer to $30. Enterprise is quoted individually and generally applies from around 150 seats. Confirm the current figures at checkout, since tier structures have been reorganised more than once.

**Is there a student discount?**
Not as a published self-serve price. Grammarly licenses institutionally through its education programme, so students at participating universities may already have free access — check with your institution first. Individual discounts are handled through third-party student verification services, and the percentages quoted on coupon sites are unreliable.
