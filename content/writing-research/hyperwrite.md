# HyperWrite: Strong Personalisation, Small Company, One Credibility Question

HyperWrite is a writing assistant with a distinctive idea at its centre: rather than producing competent generic prose, it learns how *you* write and filters output through that. In a category where most tools sound identical, that is a real differentiator.

It is built by **OthersideAI**, a small New York company. Two things about that company belong in an honest review — one reassuring, one not — and both are covered below rather than left out.

## What is HyperWrite?

HyperWrite is a personal AI writing assistant available as a web app and browser extension. It has four main parts.

**TypeAhead** is inline predictive autocomplete that suggests the rest of your sentence as you type, working across the web rather than in one editor. The **AI writing assistant** handles conversational drafting and editing. **Agent Mode**, originally launched in April 2023 and pitched then as an AI that operates your browser, navigates websites and completes tasks like filling forms. A library of **task-specific tools** covers common jobs like emails and summaries.

The feature that actually distinguishes it is the **Personal Style Profile**, sometimes presented as Custom Personas. You give it samples of your writing, it models your voice, and subsequent output is shaped to match. For anyone who has spent time rewriting AI output because it does not sound like them, this addresses the problem at the source rather than after the fact.

## Key Features

- **Personal Style Profile** — trains on your writing so output resembles your voice, the strongest reason to choose it.
- **Custom Personas** for maintaining several distinct voices, useful if you write for multiple brands.
- **TypeAhead predictive autocomplete** working inline across the web.
- **Agent Mode** — browser automation that navigates sites and completes multi-step tasks, gated to paid tiers.
- **Chrome extension** so the assistant is available wherever you type.
- **Task-specific tools** for recurring writing jobs.
- **Citations and real-time information** on paid plans.

## Pros and Cons

**What it does better than bigger rivals**

Voice matching is the genuine strength, and it is a problem the large assistants have not solved well. ChatGPT and Claude will imitate a style if you prompt them carefully every time; HyperWrite holds it persistently so you do not have to. For people publishing under their own name — newsletter writers, consultants, anyone whose readers would notice a change in register — this saves the editing pass that usually follows AI drafting.

TypeAhead is the most pleasant implementation of predictive writing available in a browser, and Agent Mode was genuinely early to browser automation, arriving well before the larger vendors shipped comparable agents.

Company health signals are positive. Headcount reportedly grew from around thirty in 2024 to roughly fifty by mid-2026, which for a small company is a good sign.

**What to weigh**

It is a small company with modest funding — around $2.8 million in a 2023 seed round led by Madrona, roughly $5.4 million disclosed in total, with no later round found. That is a fraction of what competitors raise. It has not stopped the product shipping, but it is relevant if you are betting a workflow on long-term availability.

The vendor's own site is inconsistent about what you are buying: current pricing pages describe tiers as **credit allotments**, while older plan pages describe Premium as unlimited. That contradiction is unresolved, so verify what your tier actually permits before subscribing. Reported pricing is around $19.99 monthly for Premium and $44.99 for Ultra, with annual billing lower.

Review coverage is unusually thin. I could not find meaningful Trustpilot or G2 data for HyperWrite, which is not a clean bill of health — it means the usual signals about billing conduct and support quality are simply unavailable. Model disclosure is also stale; older material names GPT-4 and Cohere, which should not be assumed current.

**The Reflection 70B episode**

In September 2024, OthersideAI released an open-source model called **Reflection 70B**, and its chief executive Matt Shumer stated publicly that it beat GPT-4o on every benchmark tested. Independent evaluators at Artificial Analysis could not reproduce the results; their testing put it **below Llama 3.1 70B, the model it was built on**. Shumer said incorrect weights had been uploaded and offered evaluators access through a private API he controlled, which did not resolve the doubts. By mid-September some evaluators were publicly using the word fraud, and Shumer addressed those accusations directly. He also disclosed that the synthetic training data came from Glaive AI, a company he had invested in.

To be fair about what this does and does not mean: the dispute concerned a separate research model, not the HyperWrite product, and no regulator or court has made any finding. Nobody's HyperWrite subscription was affected. But the episode is about **whether this company's public performance claims can be taken at face value**, and that is legitimately relevant when weighing its marketing. Read its capability claims with more scepticism than you would apply to a vendor without that history, and test the product yourself rather than relying on stated benchmarks.

## How to Use HyperWrite

1. **Start on the free tier** to test whether the style matching works on your writing specifically, since that is the entire reason to choose this over a cheaper general assistant.
2. **Feed the Personal Style Profile properly.** Give it several substantial pieces you consider representative. A thin sample produces generic output and a false impression of the tool.
3. **Create separate personas** if you write in genuinely different registers, rather than blending them into one confused profile.
4. **Install the Chrome extension**, because TypeAhead's value depends on being present where you already write.
5. **Check whether your tier is credit-limited or unlimited** before relying on it heavily, given the vendor's own inconsistent documentation.
6. **Supervise Agent Mode closely.** Browser automation acting on real websites deserves attention, and you should never leave it to complete anything involving payments or irreversible submissions unattended.
7. **Verify performance claims yourself** against your own work rather than published comparisons, which is good practice generally and warranted here specifically.

### FAQs

**What makes HyperWrite different from ChatGPT for writing?**
The Personal Style Profile. HyperWrite models your voice from samples and applies it persistently, so output resembles your writing without re-prompting each time. General assistants can imitate a style when carefully instructed, but do not hold it by default. If your main frustration with AI writing is that it does not sound like you, that is the difference.

**How much does HyperWrite cost?**
There is a free tier with limited credits and restricted features. Premium is reported at around $19.99 monthly and Ultra at around $44.99, both lower on annual billing. Note that the vendor's pages describe tiers inconsistently — some as credit allotments, others as unlimited — so confirm the limits of your specific plan at signup.

**Does HyperWrite's AI agent still work?**
Yes. Agent Mode still ships and remains gated to paid tiers. It navigates websites and completes multi-step tasks such as form filling. As with any browser agent, supervise it rather than leaving it to act unattended, particularly on anything involving payments or submissions you cannot undo.

**Is HyperWrite trustworthy?**
The product functions as described and the company appears healthy for its size. The caveat is that its parent, OthersideAI, faced serious criticism in September 2024 when its Reflection 70B model failed independent reproduction after public claims that it beat GPT-4o. No finding was ever made against the company, and the product was unaffected, but it is a reason to test capability claims yourself rather than accepting them.
