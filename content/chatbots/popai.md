# POPAI: Capable Document AI With a Billing Problem You Should Know About

POPAI does two things well: it reads documents and answers questions about them, and it turns those documents into slide decks. For students working through dense PDFs and professionals who need a presentation from a report by tomorrow, that combination is genuinely useful.

It also has the worst billing reputation of any tool covered in this directory, and that belongs in the review rather than a footnote. Both halves are true, and you should decide with both in front of you.

## What is POPAI?

POPAI is an AI document assistant. Upload a PDF and you can chat with it — asking questions, requesting summaries, pulling out specific figures, or working through a long paper section by section. Beyond reading, it generates presentations from documents or prompts, drafts written content, and produces images.

It routes across several frontier models rather than running its own, drawing on Claude Sonnet 5, Claude Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.6 Flash, GPT-5.6, and GPT-image-2 depending on the task.

Pricing has a free tier with limited daily credits, around ten presentations monthly, and watermarked exports. Pro runs roughly $9.90 to $12 monthly, cheaper annually at around $99 paid upfront, with an Unlimited tier near $40 monthly and custom enterprise pricing. Mobile in-app purchase tiers span from $4.99 weekly to $99 yearly.

That upfront annual charge is where the trouble starts.

## Key Features

- **Chat with PDFs** — upload a document and query it directly, which is faster than reading linearly when you need specific information.
- **AI presentation generation** — turns a document or prompt into a slide deck, the feature most users come for.
- **Multi-model routing** — access to Claude, Gemini, and GPT families without separate subscriptions.
- **AI writing** — drafting and editing assistance alongside the document tools.
- **Image generation** via GPT-image-2.
- **Long document handling** — copes with academic papers and lengthy reports rather than choking on them.
- **Mobile apps** on iOS and Android in addition to the web app.
- **Template variety** for presentation output.

## Pros and Cons

**Where it delivers**

Document question-answering is fast and accurate enough to change how you work through long material. For a student facing a fifty-page paper, asking targeted questions and getting cited passages back is a real time saving over reading cover to cover.

The presentation generation is the standout. Producing a structured deck from a report in one step gets you to roughly seventy percent of a finished presentation, and finishing from there is far quicker than starting from a blank slide. The multi-model access is good value at the price — reaching Claude and GPT families through one modest subscription compares well against separate plans. Mobile apps make it usable for reading on the move.

**The problems, stated plainly**

Billing is the dominant complaint and it is well documented. Users report the annual plan being charged as a single upfront payment despite pricing copy that reads as monthly, refunds being refused, and immediate downgrade to the free tier on cancellation rather than access continuing through the paid period. Trustpilot ratings sit around 1.7 stars and Google Play near 3.6, with billing the recurring theme. The pricing page has been criticised as opaque, and prices change frequently.

None of that means the product does not work — it does. It means you should treat the purchase carefully. Read exactly what you are being charged and over what period before confirming, and consider paying monthly rather than annually even at a higher effective rate, because the annual commitment is where most complaints originate. If you subscribe through the iOS or Android app, cancellation runs through Apple or Google, which some users find more reliable than the web billing flow.

Free-tier exports carry watermarks, which limits their use for anything you would share professionally.

## How to Use POPAI

1. **Start on the free tier** and test with a document that resembles your real work — a long PDF you actually need to understand.
2. **Ask specific questions rather than requesting summaries.** "What sample size did the third study use?" produces better results than "summarise this."
3. **Verify quoted figures against the source.** Document AI misreads tables and charts more often than it misreads prose, so check numbers you intend to rely on.
4. **For presentations, supply a source document** rather than a bare prompt. Decks generated from real material are substantially better than decks generated from a topic sentence.
5. **Expect to finish the deck yourself.** Treat the output as structure and first-pass content, then edit for accuracy and tone.
6. **Read the checkout screen carefully before paying.** Confirm the exact amount, the billing period, and whether you are being charged upfront for a full year.
7. **Prefer monthly billing, or subscribe through the mobile app** if you want cancellation handled by Apple or Google rather than the web flow.

### FAQs

**How much does POPAI cost?**
The free tier costs nothing but has limited daily credits, roughly ten presentations monthly, and watermarked exports. Pro is around $9.90 to $12 monthly, or approximately $99 for a year charged as a single upfront payment. An Unlimited tier sits near $40 monthly, with custom enterprise pricing above it. Mobile in-app tiers range from $4.99 weekly to $99 yearly. Prices change often, so check the current page before subscribing.

**Why does POPAI have poor billing reviews?**
The recurring complaints are that annual plans are charged in full upfront while the pricing presentation reads as monthly, that refund requests are refused, and that cancelling downgrades you to the free tier immediately rather than at the end of the paid period. Trustpilot sits near 1.7 stars, with billing dominating the negative reviews. The product itself generally works — the friction is commercial, not technical.

**Can I get a refund from POPAI?**
Users report refusals, so do not subscribe assuming you can reverse it. If you want the option of a cleaner cancellation, subscribing through the iOS or Android app routes billing through Apple or Google, whose refund processes are more predictable. Otherwise, choose monthly billing so any mistake costs one month rather than a year.

**Is POPAI good for making presentations?**
Yes, this is its strongest feature, particularly when generating from an existing document rather than a short prompt. Expect roughly seventy percent of a finished deck — the structure and initial content — and plan to edit for accuracy and tone. That is still considerably faster than building from scratch.
