# Synthesys Review: The Voice-and-Avatar Tool People Confuse With Synthesia

Before a single feature, there is one thing you need to get straight, because it trips up almost everyone: Synthesys is not Synthesia. They sound nearly identical, they both make AI media, and they are two entirely separate companies. Synthesia (at synthesia.io) is the larger, enterprise-focused avatar business; Synthesys (at synthesys.io, reviewed here) is a smaller, separate product that started in AI voice and has since grown into avatars and video.

Get that clear at the outset and the rest of this review makes sense. Mix them up and you will end up comparing the wrong prices, expecting the wrong scale, and attributing one company's reputation to the other. This piece is strictly about Synthesys.io — its own strengths, limits, and pricing — with the name clash flagged wherever it matters.

## What is Synthesys?

Synthesys, found at synthesys.io, is an AI media platform that began life in voiceover and text-to-speech. Its original strength was turning typed scripts into natural-sounding narration, and it built out from there. A "talking photo" capability — marketed under the Human Studio name — let users animate a still image into a speaking presenter, and more recently the platform has expanded into fuller AI avatars and video generation.

That history explains its character. Where some competitors were avatar-first from day one, Synthesys came up through audio, so voice generation remains a core part of what it does rather than an afterthought bolted on. Today it presents as a mid-market, all-in-one option for creators who want both a voice and a face without assembling separate tools.

One point of honesty is warranted: the corporate details behind Synthesys — who founded it, where it is headquartered, how it is funded — are not clearly or reliably documented in public sources. Rather than guess, this review leaves those blanks blank. What can be assessed is the product and its positioning, and importantly, none of Synthesia's founder, funding, or scale should be assumed to apply here.

## Key Features

- **AI voiceover and text-to-speech** — the platform's founding capability, generating spoken narration from typed text across a range of voices and languages.
- **Human Studio talking photos** — animates a still image into a speaking presenter, a distinctive feature rooted in the company's earlier work.
- **AI avatars** — presenter-style digital humans that deliver a script to camera, extending the tool beyond voice into full video.
- **Video generation** — assembles narrated, avatar-led clips for marketing, explainers, and social content without filming.
- **Multiple voices and languages** — a library of synthetic voices intended to suit different tones, audiences, and markets.
- **Script-to-video workflow** — a straightforward path from written script to finished narrated video within a single interface.
- **Web-based access** — runs in the browser, so there is no heavy desktop software to install and maintain.

## Pros and Cons

**What works well**

Synthesys earns its keep as an affordable, combined voice-and-avatar option for people who do not need enterprise machinery. Because it grew out of text-to-speech, the voiceover side is a genuine strength rather than a checkbox, and pairing that with talking photos and avatars in one place is convenient for solo creators and small teams. The Human Studio talking-photo feature is a nice differentiator, letting you build a presenter from a single image. For mid-market budgets, having voice and face under one subscription is a practical draw.

**Where it falls short**

The single biggest pitfall is not a product flaw at all — it is the constant confusion with Synthesia. Reviews, comparisons, and even casual recommendations routinely blur the two, so buyers must be careful they are evaluating and paying for the right one. Beyond that, Synthesys is the smaller player: it does not carry Synthesia's enterprise scale, and its top-end avatar realism generally will not match the market leaders. The thin public record on the company behind it is also worth noting for buyers who like to know who they are dealing with.

**A word on choosing between the two**

If you specifically want a low-cost tool that does voice and avatars together for social and marketing content, Synthesys is a reasonable fit. If you need enterprise-grade avatars, heavy governance, and large-team features, you are probably thinking of Synthesia — a different company — and should evaluate it on its own terms.
