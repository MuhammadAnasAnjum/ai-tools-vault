# ChatBot.com: Visual Flows, Trained Answers, and a Metered Bill

ChatBot.com comes from Text, Inc., the company behind LiveChat — and the first thing to understand is that the two products are sold and billed separately. Wanting both means two subscriptions. That trips up a surprising number of buyers who assume the chatbot is a LiveChat feature.

What you get for the separate bill is a mature visual flow builder paired with AI that answers from your own content. The combination is genuinely useful. The pricing model is where you need to do arithmetic.

## What is ChatBot.com?

ChatBot.com is a no-code platform for building customer-facing chatbots. It takes two approaches and lets you blend them.

The **Visual Builder** handles deterministic conversation flows — the branching paths where you want precise control over what the bot says, including A/B testing of up to five variants of a flow. This is the traditional, predictable half.

The **AI half** trains on your existing material. A scanner ingests your website, help centre, and documentation — up to 2,000 pages per run and 5,000 in total — and the bot then answers questions from that material. **AI Knowledge** manages those sources, and a Training panel surfaces queries the bot handled badly so you can correct them.

Pricing changed during 2026, and this matters: current tiers are per seat *and* cap AI resolutions. Essential is $19 per user monthly billed annually ($25 monthly) with 10 AI resolutions per month. Growth is $79 per user annually ($99 monthly) with 200. Enterprise is custom. Overage packs of 50 resolutions run about $49.50, roughly $0.99 each. Legacy flat tiers around $52, $149, and $499 are still widely republished and are out of date.

## Key Features

- **Visual flow builder** — drag-and-drop conversation design with conditional logic, for cases where you need exact control.
- **Website and help-centre scanner** — trains the bot on your own content, up to 2,000 pages per run.
- **AI Knowledge management** — organise and update the sources your bot answers from.
- **Training panel** — surfaces unhandled or badly answered questions so you can fix gaps deliberately.
- **A/B testing** — compare up to five variants of a flow, which is unusual in this category.
- **Personalisation** — uses name, email, and purchase history to tailor replies.
- **Product recommendations** — suited to e-commerce catalogues.
- **Integrations** — LiveChat, Messenger, Slack, Shopify, WordPress, Zapier, plus a REST API.

## Pros and Cons

**Where it earns its keep**

The blend of deterministic flows and trained AI answers is the real strength. Many competitors force a choice — rigid decision trees or an AI that improvises. Here you can script the paths that must be exact, such as returns policy or order status, and let AI handle the long tail of everything else. For a support team that has been burned by an over-creative bot, that control matters.

The Training panel is a quietly excellent feature. Seeing the questions your bot fumbled is the fastest route to improving it, and it doubles as a map of the gaps in your documentation. A/B testing flows is rare in no-code builders and genuinely useful for conversion work. Setup requires no engineering, and the e-commerce integrations are solid.

**Where the model works against you**

The pricing structure is the main problem, because it multiplies two variables. You pay per seat *and* you are capped on AI resolutions, which makes forecasting difficult. The Essential tier's 10 resolutions per month is barely a demonstration — any real site will exhaust it in a day, so the practical entry point is Growth at $79 per user annually. Overage at roughly $0.99 per resolution adds up quickly on a busy site.

There is no permanent free tier, only a trial, which makes evaluation harder than with competitors that let you build and test for free indefinitely. The scanner's page caps constrain large documentation sets. And there is no consumer mobile app, so managing conversations means the web app.

## How to Use ChatBot.com

1. **Start the trial** and connect your website so the scanner can ingest your existing content before you build anything.
2. **Audit what it learned.** Ask the bot ten real customer questions and see where the answers come from. Weak sources produce weak answers, and fixing your help docs pays off more than tuning the bot.
3. **Script your must-be-exact paths in the Visual Builder** — refunds, shipping, order status, anything with a policy behind it. Do not leave these to AI improvisation.
4. **Let AI cover the long tail** of questions too varied to script.
5. **Set a clear handoff to a human**, and connect LiveChat if you use it — remembering that it is a separate subscription.
6. **Check the Training panel weekly** and fix the gaps it surfaces at the source.
7. **Meter your resolutions before committing annually.** Track a fortnight of real traffic against your tier's cap so you know whether overage will dominate your bill.

### FAQs

**Is ChatBot.com the same as LiveChat?**
No. Both come from Text, Inc., but they are separate products with separate subscriptions. LiveChat is human-agent live chat; ChatBot.com is the automated bot builder. They integrate cleanly, and many teams run both — which means paying for both.

**How much does ChatBot.com cost?**
Current tiers are Essential at $19 per user monthly billed annually ($25 monthly) with 10 AI resolutions per month, Growth at $79 per user annually ($99 monthly) with 200 resolutions, and custom Enterprise pricing. Additional resolutions come in packs of 50 for around $49.50. Legacy flat pricing near $52, $149, and $499 appears in many older articles and is no longer accurate — check the official pricing page.

**Is there a free plan?**
No, only a free trial. If a permanently free tier is important for evaluating or running a low-volume bot, competitors including Chatbase offer one.

**ChatBot.com or Chatbase — which should I choose?**
Choose ChatBot.com if you need tight control over scripted conversation paths, A/B testing, and e-commerce integrations. Choose Chatbase if you mainly want an agent that answers from your documentation with minimal setup and prefer a free tier to start on. ChatBot.com is the more controllable tool; Chatbase is the faster one to stand up.
