# AI Chatbots and Tools: Which One Actually Fits What You Do

The honest answer to "which AI chatbot is best" is that the question is wrong. These ten products have diverged sharply. Some are general assistants competing head-on; others solve a narrow problem well and are useless outside it. Paying for the wrong one is the most common mistake people make here.

This category covers nineteen tools reviewed individually, with pricing and status verified in August 2026. Below is how they actually divide up.

## The general assistants

**ChatGPT**, **Claude**, and **Google Gemini** are the three you would realistically pick between as your main assistant, and the deciding factor is usually where your work already lives rather than benchmark scores. Gemini has direct access to your Gmail and Drive. Copilot works inside Word and Excel on your real files. Claude handles long documents and writing with less editing needed afterwards. ChatGPT has the widest feature set and the deepest ecosystem of anything here.

**Microsoft Copilot** belongs in this group but is priced differently — the standalone Copilot Pro tier was discontinued, and consumer access now arrives bundled with Microsoft 365 subscriptions. If you already pay for Office, you may already have it.

## The research specialist

**Perplexity** is not really competing with the above. It is an answer engine: search the live web, synthesise a response, cite every claim. For factual research it is faster and cleaner than either search or a general chatbot. For writing, brainstorming, or coding it is the wrong tool. Note the accuracy caveat in the full review — cited answers are easy to verify but the citations themselves are not always sound.

## The aggregator

**Poe** sells access to many models — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and others plus image and video generators — under one subscription metered in compute points. It is the cheapest way to compare models against your own real prompts before committing to a direct subscription. It gives you the models but not the native features that surround them.

## The build-it-yourself tool

**Chatbase** is the outlier: you are not the end user, your customers are. Point it at your documentation and it produces a support agent you embed on your site. Budget by modelling your ticket volume against its credit system rather than by reading the plan price, because model choice can swing the effective cost several times over.

## The conversation-first products

**Character.AI** and **Pi AI** optimise for talking rather than tasks. Character.AI is built for roleplay and companionship, and is now a much more restricted platform than older reviews describe — under-18 users lost open-ended chat in November 2025 following litigation that settled in January 2026, and several regulators have since acted. Pi is the most naturally conversational assistant here and completely free with no paid tier, but it is in maintenance mode after Microsoft acqui-hired Inflection's founding team, and it cannot code, analyse data, or handle files.

## The European alternative

**Mistral Le Chat**, now renamed **Vibe**, is the most credible non-American option: its own frontier models, Paris-hosted data residency, a no-telemetry mode, and openly licensed weights that make self-hosting genuinely possible. Pro undercuts the $20 standard at $14.99/month with a coding agent included. The trade-off is a smaller ecosystem and less community documentation.

## Build-your-own bot platforms

Four tools here are for making a bot rather than talking to one. **Chatbase** is the fastest route from a folder of documentation to a working support widget. **ChatBot.com**, from the LiveChat group, pairs scripted visual flows with trained AI answers, which suits teams who need certain paths to be exact — note it bills separately from LiveChat and has no free plan. **Coze** is the most powerful of the four and the only one whose core you can self-host, since ByteDance released Coze Studio under Apache 2.0. **Gleap** sits slightly apart, capturing in-app bug reports with session replay and using AI agents to triage them and even open pull requests.

## Coding, writing and everyday work

**GitHub Copilot** is the default AI coding assistant, free for verified students and worth reading about specifically for its security research and unresolved licensing litigation. Do not confuse it with Microsoft Copilot above — different products, different bills.

**QuillBot** rewrites text you have already written, which makes it excellent for non-native English writers and a genuine academic integrity question for students; the review covers where institutions draw the line. **POPAI** reads PDFs and turns documents into slide decks capably, though its billing practices draw consistent complaints that we cover rather than skip. **Taskade** is a multi-view project workspace whose AI agents and app builder have become its main investment, to the mild frustration of users who wanted a task manager.

## Two tools you should not sign up for

Both of these are still recommended by directories and listicles, which is exactly why they are worth documenting.

**Respell** no longer exists. Its team joined Salesforce and the standalone product was shut down, with customers migrated to Lindy — though its own documentation still describes pricing in the present tense. Our page covers what happened and what to use instead.

**WordStream's** paid Advisor platform was discontinued after Gannett folded the brand into LOCALiQ. The website, blog, and free Google and Facebook Ads graders are genuinely live and useful; the subscription software is gone. Any page quoting "$49/month and up" is recycling pre-2022 information.

## How to choose without wasting money

Start free where you can. Most products here have a free tier, and several are capable enough that you may never need to pay — the exceptions are ChatBot.com and Gleap, which offer trials only. Then pick based on three questions: where do your files and daily work already live, is your main task writing and analysis or research and fact-finding, and do you need one strong assistant or breadth across several models? Answering those honestly narrows nineteen options to one or two.

Two warnings apply across the whole category. Pricing, model names, and usage limits change every few months, so much of the advice published about these tools describes versions that no longer exist. And metered billing is now the norm rather than the exception — credits, compute points, AI resolutions, and separately-billed tokens all mean the headline price is rarely your actual bill. Model your real usage before committing annually.

Every review here carries a verification date. Checking the vendor's own pricing page before you subscribe remains worth the thirty seconds.
