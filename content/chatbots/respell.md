# Respell Has Shut Down: What Happened, and Where to Go Instead

If you landed here looking for Respell pricing or a signup link, the short answer is that there is nothing to sign up for. **Respell was shut down as a standalone product** and its team joined Salesforce to work on Agentforce. respell.ai now serves only a transition notice.

This page exists because a lot of directories and listicles still recommend Respell as though it were live, and its own documentation site still describes plans in the present tense. That is stale content, not evidence of an operating service. Here is the accurate account and what to use instead.

## What was Respell?

Respell was a no-code platform for building AI-powered workflows, which it called **Spells**. You assembled a sequence visually — take an input, run it through a language model, branch on the result, write the output somewhere — without writing code.

It ran on models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Cohere, and integrated with the tools workflow automation usually touches: Gmail, Notion, Slack, Airtable, and Salesforce. The pitch was aimed at non-technical operators who wanted AI in their processes without engineering support, and it was a reasonable execution of that idea.

The company was founded in 2022 in San Francisco and raised approximately $5 million.

## Why it shut down

The ending was an acqui-hire rather than a product acquisition — the distinction matters for understanding why nothing survived. Salesforce brought the Respell team in to work on **Agentforce**, its agent platform. Respell's own announcement stated plainly that the company would be "shutting down Respell as a standalone product on March 1st."

Because the team was the asset, the product was not maintained or folded into Salesforce for existing customers. It simply stopped. Respell partnered with **Lindy** to migrate customers' Spells, since there was no self-serve export path.

One note on dating: the announcement page carries a January 2024 date, implying a March 2024 sunset, which sits awkwardly against Salesforce's Agentforce branding timeline. The shutdown itself is confirmed; treat the precise date as approximate.

## What to use instead

The no-code AI workflow space has consolidated considerably since Respell launched, and the alternatives are stronger than what it offered.

**Lindy** is the most direct successor, having handled Respell's own customer migration. It builds AI agents that handle email, scheduling, and multi-step processes, and it is the natural first stop if you are moving existing Spells.

**n8n** is the choice if you want durability above all else. It is open source and self-hostable, with strong AI nodes alongside hundreds of conventional integrations. The relevant advantage here is that a self-hostable tool cannot be taken away from you by an acqui-hire — which, after Respell, is not a theoretical concern.

**Make** and **Zapier** both added substantial AI capability to mature automation platforms. Neither is AI-first, but both have far larger integration libraries and the commercial stability that comes with scale.

**Coze** is worth considering for agent-style workflows rather than linear automations, and its core is open source under Apache 2.0, so it carries the same insurance as n8n.

For teams already inside the Salesforce ecosystem, **Agentforce** is where the Respell team's work actually went, though it is an enterprise platform rather than a lightweight tool for individual operators.

## How to migrate away from a discontinued tool

The Respell situation is a useful case study, because this will happen again to some tool you depend on.

1. **Document what the workflow actually does** before touching the replacement — inputs, logic, outputs, and who depends on it. Rebuilding from memory guarantees you lose an edge case.
2. **Export everything you can while access remains.** Respell had no self-serve export, which is exactly why you should not rely on a vendor providing one later.
3. **Rebuild your highest-value workflow first** on the new platform, and run both in parallel if the old one still works.
4. **Prefer tools with an export path or open-source core** when choosing the replacement. This is the concrete lesson: n8n and Coze can be self-hosted, so a corporate acquisition cannot strand you.
5. **Avoid annual prepayment with early-stage vendors.** A startup with $5 million raised and no clear revenue path is a real acqui-hire candidate.
6. **Keep a written record of your integrations and credentials** so rebuilding is configuration work rather than archaeology.
7. **Re-test the outputs.** A rebuilt workflow that runs is not the same as one that produces correct results.

### FAQs

**Is Respell still available in 2026?**
No. Respell was shut down as a standalone product and its team joined Salesforce to work on Agentforce. The respell.ai homepage serves a transition notice. Its documentation subdomain still describes pricing and plans in the present tense, and many tool directories still list it as active — both are stale, not evidence that you can sign up.

**What happened to my Spells?**
Respell partnered with Lindy to migrate customer workflows, as there was no self-serve export function. If you still have Spells you have not moved and can access an account, migrating or documenting them promptly is the sensible move — shutdown-era access paths tend to degrade over time.

**Was Respell acquired by Salesforce?**
Functionally it was an acqui-hire: Salesforce brought in the team, and the product was discontinued rather than absorbed. That is why existing customers did not transition into a Salesforce product and were pointed toward a third-party migration instead.

**What is the closest replacement for Respell?**
Lindy is the most direct equivalent and handled Respell's own migration. If you want protection against this happening again, n8n is open source and self-hostable with strong AI nodes. Make and Zapier offer more integrations and greater commercial stability, though they are automation platforms with AI added rather than AI-first tools.
