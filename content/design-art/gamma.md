# Gamma in 2026: Fast AI Decks, Sites and Docs — and the Slop Question

Gamma is an AI tool that turns a prompt or an outline into a finished presentation, website or document in minutes. Instead of the slide-by-slide grind of traditional deck software, you describe what you want and Gamma assembles a styled, editable result you can refine. That speed has made it one of the fastest-growing tools in its category, with a reported 70 million users and more than 600,000 paying customers.

We place Gamma in Design & Art with a caveat, and it is worth stating plainly: this is a productivity tool that happens to produce visual output. It is closer to presentation and document software than to fine-art generation, and its job is to communicate information attractively rather than to create art. If you arrive expecting a creative image generator, you will be in the wrong place; if you need decks, one-pagers and simple sites made faster, you are in the right one.

## What is Gamma?

Gamma is a generation platform for presentations, websites and documents. You give it a topic, a prompt or an existing outline, and it produces a set of styled cards — Gamma's flexible take on slides — complete with layouts, images and formatting that you can then edit. Themes and templates control the look, smart layouts arrange content automatically, and the same material can be reflowed between a deck, a document and a web page. It is run by an independent company led by founder and chief executive Grant Lee, and is notably lean, operating with only around fifty employees.

The current flagship is Gamma 3.0, launched in September 2025, which added a Gamma Agent that can research the web and cite sources, instant restyling, feedback on your deck, an API with Zapier and Make support, expanded smart layouts and diagrams, team and brand features, and a premium Gamma Ultra tier. The company's trajectory is well documented: a $68 million Series B at a $2.1 billion valuation was announced in November 2025, led by Andreessen Horowitz with Accel and Uncork, and Gamma has said it passed $100 million in annual recurring revenue, up from about $24 million in 2024, while being profitable since 2023.

## Key Features

- **AI deck generation** — turn a prompt, topic or outline into a styled, editable presentation in minutes.
- **AI websites** — generate a simple, publishable website from the same content-first workflow.
- **AI documents** — produce formatted documents and one-pagers without manual layout work.
- **Gamma Agent** — research the web with citations and act on instructions, added in Gamma 3.0.
- **Templates and themes** — a catalogue of looks, plus instant restyling to change a whole deck's appearance at once.
- **Export options** — send decks out to PowerPoint or PDF, with the formatting quirks noted below.
- **Analytics** — see how shared decks and pages are viewed and engaged with.
- **Team and brand controls** — shared brand settings and collaboration features for organisations.

## Pros and Cons

**What works well**

Gamma's core promise holds up: going from a blank page to a complete, decent-looking draft in minutes is a real time-saver, and for anyone who dreads building decks it removes most of the friction. The content-first approach — write the substance, let the tool handle layout — suits people who think in words rather than shapes. The breadth is unusual, with decks, documents and websites from one workflow, and the Gamma 3.0 additions, particularly the citation-capable Agent and instant restyling, address real gaps. The commercial signals are strong too: profitability since 2023, rapid revenue growth and a large paying user base suggest a product people keep using rather than try once. The free tier lets you evaluate it properly before paying.

**Where it falls short**

The recurring criticism is quality, and it is fair to air it. Reviewers frequently describe Gamma's output as generic or repetitive — the complaint that "every deck looks the same" comes up often — and note that AI-generated images can be distorted even on paid plans. The presenting experience is called weak and static by some, customisation is seen as limited compared with full design tools, and PowerPoint exports can arrive with messy formatting that needs fixing. There are scattered complaints about support and account access as well. Two caveats balance this: many of the harshest critics are competing tools with an interest in the comparison, so weigh the source; and the "AI slop" charge applies to careless use more than to the tool itself — output improves markedly when you edit rather than accept the first draft. Pricing is freemium and credit-based, with a reported Pro plan around $18 per month and a premium Ultra tier around $100 per month; those figures are second-hand, so confirm them directly.

## How to Use Gamma

1. **Sign up for a free account** — start on the free tier before deciding whether a paid plan is worth it.
2. **Choose a format** — decide whether you are making a presentation, a document or a website, as the workflow adapts.
3. **Give it a prompt or outline** — describe your topic or paste an existing outline; a clearer brief yields a better first draft.
4. **Pick a theme** — choose a look from the template catalogue, which you can restyle instantly later.
5. **Generate the draft** — let Gamma build the cards, then read through the whole thing before editing anything.
6. **Edit for substance** — this is where quality is won or lost: rewrite generic text, replace weak or distorted images, and cut filler.
7. **Use the Agent if needed** — call on the Gamma Agent to research and add cited detail where your draft is thin.
8. **Present or export** — present from Gamma directly, publish a site, or export to PowerPoint or PDF, checking the formatting after export.

### FAQs

**Is Gamma free?**
Yes, there is a free tier that lets you try the core generation features. Paid plans are reported at around $18 per month for Pro and around $100 per month for the premium Ultra tier introduced with Gamma 3.0, but those figures are second-hand and the model is credit-based, so confirm the current tiers on the vendor page.

**Is Gamma any good, or is it "AI slop"?**
Both the criticism and the praise are warranted. Output can look generic if you accept the first draft, and AI images sometimes come out distorted, but results improve substantially when you edit the substance and replace weak visuals. Remember too that many harsh reviews come from competing tools, so weigh the source before taking the verdict at face value.

**Can I export to PowerPoint?**
Yes, Gamma exports to PowerPoint and PDF, but reviewers note the PowerPoint formatting can arrive messy and need tidying. If a polished PPT file is essential — for a client's own template, say — budget time to fix the export rather than assuming it will transfer cleanly.

**Is Gamma a design tool or a productivity tool?**
It is closer to productivity. It generates presentations, documents and websites to communicate information, not artwork, so we treat it as design-adjacent rather than as a creative art generator. If your goal is fast, presentable business content, it fits; if you want original illustration or design, it is the wrong category of tool.
