# Cursor Review: The Agentic Code Editor Developers Argue About

Cursor has become one of the most talked-about tools in software development, and not only for its features. It is an AI code editor that started life as a fork of VS Code and grew into a standalone agentic environment shipping its own models. This review looks at what it does well, where it frustrates people, and the business drama surrounding it.

We will cover the editor itself, the pricing change that upset its community in 2025, and a reported acquisition that would be extraordinary if it closes. The aim is a balanced picture, so you can decide whether Cursor earns a place in your daily work.

## What is Cursor?

Cursor is an AI-first code editor built by Anysphere Inc., a San Francisco company founded in 2022 by four MIT classmates: Michael Truell, who serves as CEO, along with Sualeh Asif, Arvid Lunnemark and Aman Sanger. Because it began as a fork of Microsoft's VS Code, the interface feels familiar to anyone who has used that editor, and many extensions and keybindings carry over. By 2026 it had matured into a fully agentic IDE that can also run models Anysphere trains itself.

The company has grown quickly. It raised a Series C of roughly 900 million dollars in June 2025 at a valuation near 9 to 10 billion dollars, then a Series D of 2.3 billion dollars in November 2025 that reportedly valued it at around 29 billion, with Nvidia cited among backers. It was also reported to have passed one billion dollars in annual recurring revenue late in 2025. In other words, this is no longer a scrappy side project but a heavily funded platform with serious momentum behind it.

## Key Features

- **Tab** — Cursor's signature proprietary autocomplete that predicts multi-line edits rather than single tokens.
- **Agent Mode** — an autonomous loop that reads your code, edits files and runs terminal commands to complete a described task.
- **Parallel agents** — Cursor 3.0 in April 2026 added running several agents at once in isolated git worktrees.
- **Composer** — multi-file editing plus a first-party fast model line, with a version reported around 2.5 in May 2026.
- **Background Agents** — cloud virtual-machine agents that branch your repository and open pull requests.
- **Bugbot** — an agentic pull-request reviewer for GitHub, launched in July 2025.
- **Model choice** — supports frontier third-party models such as Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's GPT-5, alongside Cursor's own Composer models.
- **Broad reach** — MCP support, a command-line and headless mode, a JetBrains plugin and mobile apps.

## Pros and Cons

**What works well**

Tab is the feature people miss most when they leave. Its multi-line prediction anticipates where you are going in a way ordinary autocomplete does not, and it saves keystrokes all day. Agent Mode is capable for larger tasks, reading across a project and making coordinated edits, and running several agents in parallel worktrees suits developers juggling multiple threads of work. Because the base is VS Code, onboarding is quick and the extension ecosystem largely carries over. Offering both frontier models and Cursor's own faster Composer line lets you trade quality against speed and cost.

**Where it falls short**

What people grumble about most is the bill. The usage-based model means heavy "max mode" refactors can consume a monthly allowance far quicker than people expect, and the value you get varies with how you work. Agents are impressive but not infallible; they can confidently produce changes that look right and are not, so review remains essential. There is also a strategic wrinkle worth knowing: investors have flagged Cursor's heavy reliance on Anthropic's models as a risk to its unit economics, which is part of why the company invests in its own models. If you already live happily inside stock VS Code with a cheaper assistant, the upgrade may not justify itself.

**The pricing controversy and a reported acquisition**

Two events are worth understanding before you commit. In June 2025 the 20-dollar Pro plan shifted from a fixed allowance of 500 requests to a usage-based credit pool marketed as "unlimited" but effectively capped, and heavy refactors could burn through a month's credits fast. The change drew a large backlash on Reddit and community forums, and on 7 July 2025 CEO Michael Truell publicly apologised for the poor communication and unexpected charges; existing users were grandfathered onto their prior terms. Separately, in June 2026 it was widely reported by outlets including CNBC, Forbes and Fortune that SpaceX had agreed to acquire Anysphere in an all-stock deal valued near 60 billion dollars, framed as the largest venture-backed startup acquisition ever and expected to close in the third quarter of 2026. Treat that as reported and pending rather than settled; it would be an extraordinary outcome, and the terms or timing could still change.

## How to Use Cursor

1. **Download and install** — grab Cursor for your operating system and open it; the layout mirrors VS Code.
2. **Import your setup** — bring across VS Code extensions, themes and keybindings during onboarding.
3. **Start on the Hobby tier** — the free plan lets you form a view before paying anything.
4. **Learn Tab first** — accept and reject its multi-line suggestions until you trust its rhythm.
5. **Try Agent Mode on a small task** — describe a change and watch it edit files and run commands, then review the diff.
6. **Pick the right model** — switch between frontier models and Composer depending on whether you want quality or speed.
7. **Watch your usage** — check the credit meter during heavy refactors so a large task does not surprise you.
8. **Use Bugbot on pull requests** — let the reviewer flag routine issues before a colleague reads the diff.

### FAQs

**Is Cursor free?**
There is a free tier named "Hobby". Paid plans are reported to include Pro around 20 dollars a month, a Pro+ tier around 60 dollars, an Ultra tier around 200 dollars, plus per-user Teams and custom Enterprise options. These figures are second-hand, so confirm the current tiers on the vendor page.

**Is Cursor just VS Code with AI?**
It started as a VS Code fork, so it feels similar, but by 2026 it is a standalone agentic IDE with its own autonomous agents, parallel worktrees and first-party models. The familiarity is a convenience, not the whole story.

**Did SpaceX buy Cursor?**
As of August 2026 it was reported that SpaceX agreed to acquire Anysphere, Cursor's maker, in an all-stock deal worth roughly 60 billion dollars, expected to close in the third quarter of 2026. Reports described it as pending rather than complete, so verify the current status before treating it as done.

**Why were users upset about pricing?**
