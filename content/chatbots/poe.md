# Poe: Many AI Models Behind One Subscription — With a Catch

Poe's pitch is appealing to anyone who has considered paying for three AI subscriptions at once. Instead of separate bills for ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini, you pay Poe and reach all of them — plus image and video generators — through one interface.

The idea is sound. The economics are where you need to read carefully, and where Poe's own trajectory has become part of the story.

## What is Poe?

Poe, built by Quora, is an aggregator that provides access to many AI models through a single account. Rather than running its own frontier model, it routes your messages to models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, DeepSeek, and others, and adds image and video generation from a range of providers including Sora, Veo, Runway, Luma, and Pika.

Access is metered in **compute points**. Every plan comes with a monthly allowance, and each model charges a different number of points per message — a lightweight model costs very little, a frontier reasoning model or a video generation considerably more. You spend from one shared pool however you like.

Poe also hosts more than a million user-created bots, with a creator programme that pays people whose bots get used. Developers can reach it through a Python SDK and REST APIs compatible with OpenAI and Anthropic formats.

One piece of context worth knowing: in April 2026, Quora CEO Adam D'Angelo announced layoffs concentrated on Poe teams, explaining that Quora's profitable Q&A business would stop subsidising Poe and that Poe must now fund its own growth. Poe was still hiring engineers earlier in the year, so this reads as a correction rather than a shutdown — but it is a reason to avoid over-committing to an annual plan.

## Key Features

- **Many models, one login** — frontier text models from several vendors alongside image and video generators, switchable mid-conversation.
- **Shared compute pool** — allocate your allowance however you want rather than being locked into one vendor's limits.
- **Model comparison** — send the same prompt to different models and see which handles it best, genuinely useful when evaluating tools.
- **Over a million custom bots** — community-built assistants for specific tasks, plus the ability to build and share your own.
- **Creator monetisation** — earn from bots you build, priced per message.
- **Developer APIs** — a native Python SDK plus OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible REST endpoints, so existing code often works with minimal changes.
- **Cheap entry point** — paid access starts at $4.99/month, well below the $20 standard elsewhere.
- **Cross-platform** — web, iOS, Android, and desktop.

## Pros and Cons

**The genuine appeal**

For evaluation and experimentation, Poe is excellent. If you want to know whether Claude or Gemini handles your particular kind of work better, running both against real prompts for a month at $4.99 is far cheaper than two $20 subscriptions. The breadth is real, especially on media generation, where reaching several video models through one account would otherwise mean several signups. Custom bots cover a lot of narrow use cases you would otherwise prompt from scratch. And for developers, API compatibility with both OpenAI and Anthropic formats makes it easy to test model swaps.

**The catch, stated plainly**

The compute points system confuses nearly everyone at first, because each bot prices messages differently from a shared pool, making it hard to know what a conversation will cost until you have spent it. The more serious issue is allowance shrinkflation at stable prices. Reports indicate the roughly $19.99 tier's monthly allowance fell from around 1 million points to 660,000, and the top tier from 12.5 million to 8.25 million, without a price change. Poe adjusts allowances without announcement, which means any pricing table — including this one — needs a visible date on it.

Reported tiers run approximately: Starter $4.99, Premium $19.99, Premium Plus $49.99, Pro $99.99, and Pro Max $249.99, with around 17% off for annual billing and additional points purchasable at roughly $30 per million. Treat those figures as indicative and check poe.com directly.

There is also a straightforward value question. If you know you want Claude, subscribing to Claude directly gives you Claude Code, Projects, and the full native feature set. Poe gives you the model but not the surrounding product. Aggregators are best for breadth, not depth.

## How to Use Poe

1. **Sign up at poe.com** and use the free tier first — the daily point allowance is enough to understand how the system feels.
2. **Learn the point costs before you spend.** Check what each model charges per message so a few video generations do not consume your month.
3. **Match the model to the task.** Cheap fast models for drafting and simple questions; save frontier reasoning models for problems that need them.
4. **Run comparisons deliberately.** Send an identical, realistic prompt to three models and judge the outputs — this is Poe's best use.
5. **Search existing bots** before building your own. Someone has usually already made a bot for your niche task.
6. **Prefer monthly billing.** Given the layoffs and the pattern of allowance changes, annual commitment carries more risk here than with first-party subscriptions.
7. **Track your consumption weekly** for the first month, so you can size your tier from evidence rather than guessing.

### FAQs

**How do Poe's compute points work?**
Each plan includes a monthly point allowance, and every model charges a different number of points per message based on how expensive it is to run. A short exchange with a small model costs very little; a video generation or a long frontier-model reasoning task costs substantially more. You draw from one shared pool, so budgeting is entirely up to you — which is both the flexibility and the confusion.

**Is Poe cheaper than subscribing to ChatGPT and Claude separately?**
It can be, if you are a light-to-moderate user who wants variety. Two direct subscriptions cost $40/month, while Poe's mid tier is around $19.99. But heavy users often find the allowance runs short, and direct subscriptions include native features Poe cannot offer. Poe wins on breadth per dollar; direct subscriptions win on depth.

**Is Poe shutting down?**
There is no indication of a shutdown. Quora announced layoffs affecting Poe teams in April 2026 with the stated aim of making Poe fund its own growth rather than being subsidised, and the company had been hiring for Poe earlier in the year. The product is operating. The sensible response is to bill monthly rather than annually.

**Can I use Poe for commercial work?**
Yes, though review both Poe's terms and those of the underlying model providers, particularly for image and video generation where licensing varies by model. If commercial rights matter to your output, confirm them per model rather than assuming they are uniform across the platform.
