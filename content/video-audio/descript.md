# Descript Review: The Editor That Treats Your Words as the Timeline

Most video and audio editors ask you to think in waveforms and frames. Descript starts somewhere stranger and, for a lot of people, far more comfortable: it transcribes your recording and lets you edit the media by editing the text. Delete a sentence in the transcript and the matching audio and video disappear with it. For podcasters and talking-head creators who would rather read than scrub a timeline, that single idea changes the entire rhythm of editing.

This review covers what Descript does unusually well, where it frustrates, and two developments worth knowing before you commit: a change of chief executive in 2025 and a repricing that reshaped how you pay. Both are recent enough that older write-ups miss them, and both feed directly into the decision.

## What is Descript?

Descript is a transcript-based audio and video editor built by the company of the same name. Founder Andrew Mason led it for years before handing the chief executive role to Laura Burkhauser around August 2025, moving into a chairman-style position himself. The company drew wide attention when an OpenAI Startup Fund-led round backed it in 2022, and it later folded in the remote-recording tool SquadCast, sharpening its appeal to podcasters who record guests down a line.

The core concept is worth restating because it really is different. When you import a recording, Descript produces a transcript, and that text becomes your editing surface. Cutting words trims the audio; rearranging paragraphs reorders the video. It reduces editing to something close to word processing, which is why it has become a default recommendation for spoken-word content rather than heavily visual, effects-driven work.

Around that idea sits a stack of AI tooling — voice cloning, audio cleanup, filler-word removal — that turns a single app into most of a podcast and video studio. It runs largely in the cloud, syncing projects so a collaborator can pick up where you left off.

## Key Features

- **Transcript-based editing** — edit the words and the media follows, the feature that defines the whole product.
- **Overdub** — a voice-cloning tool that recreates your own voice, so you can mend a fumbled line by typing the correction.
- **Filler-word removal** — automatically finds and strips "um", "uh", and similar verbal tics across a whole recording in one pass.
- **Studio Sound** — an audio-cleanup pass that lifts muddy or noisy recordings towards a cleaner, more broadcast-like tone.
- **Multitrack editing** — handles several audio and video tracks together, suited to interviews and multi-speaker shows.
- **Screen recording** — captures your screen and camera for tutorials, demos, and software walkthroughs without a separate tool.
- **AI eye contact** — nudges your gaze towards the camera when you were reading from notes off to one side.
- **Publishing and export** — hosts and shares finished episodes or exports to the formats each platform expects.

## Pros and Cons

**What works well**

The transcript workflow is genuinely transformative for spoken content. If your video is mostly people talking, editing by text is faster, less fiddly, and far kinder to newcomers than a conventional timeline. Filler-word removal alone can save an hour on a rambling recording, and Studio Sound rescues audio that would otherwise need a separate cleanup tool. Overdub is a small marvel when you need to fix a single mistaken word without re-recording, and the SquadCast heritage means remote interviews land in the same place you edit them. As an all-in-one home for podcasts and talking-head video, few tools match its coherence.

**Where it falls short**

Descript is built around speech, so anyone chasing rich visual effects, precise colour grading, or complex motion work will quickly hit its ceiling and be happier in a dedicated video suite. Being cloud-centred, it also leans on a solid connection and can feel sluggish with very large projects. The sharper recent complaint, though, is about cost. On 24 September 2025 the company moved to a model built on "media minutes" plus separate "AI credits", and a portion of users found the new structure confusing and, for their usage, more expensive than what it replaced. It is worth mapping your real monthly volume against the tiers before you assume a plan will fit.

**A note on balanced sourcing**

Some of the harsher comparisons circulating online originate with Sonix, a competing transcription service, so treat those particular criticisms with mild caution rather than as neutral verdicts. The pricing frustration, by contrast, comes from Descript's own user base and is the more reliable signal.

## How to Use Descript

1. **Start a project** — sign in at descript.com and create a project, then import existing media or record straight into the app.
2. **Record or upload** — bring in your audio or video, or use the built-in recorder or SquadCast-style remote capture for guests.
3. **Let it transcribe** — Descript generates a transcript automatically; give it a quick read and correct any misheard names or terms.
4. **Edit the text** — cut, rearrange, and tidy by editing the words, watching the media reshape itself to match as you go.
5. **Remove the filler** — run filler-word removal, then review its picks so you keep the pauses that give speech its natural rhythm.
6. **Clean the audio** — apply Studio Sound where recordings are noisy, and use Overdub sparingly to patch small verbal slips.
7. **Add the visuals** — layer screen recordings, captions, and eye-contact correction, keeping the look consistent across an episode.
8. **Export or publish** — play the whole piece through, check your minute and credit usage, then export or publish to your chosen platform.

### FAQs

**Is Descript free?**
There is a free tier, and it is enough to learn the transcript workflow and produce short pieces, though it caps your usage and reserves the heavier AI tools for paid plans. Because billing now turns on media minutes and AI credits, confirm exactly what the free tier and each paid tier include on Descript's pricing page rather than trusting a summary.

**What actually changed with the 2025 pricing?**
On 24 September 2025 Descript shifted to charging around a pool of "media minutes" alongside separate "AI credits", rather than the simpler earlier structure. Some users welcomed the flexibility; others found it harder to predict and, for their volume, dearer. Check the current numbers and allowances directly, as reported figures of roughly ~$16, ~$24, and ~$50 monthly tiers are second-hand and subject to change.

**Is Descript good for video, or just podcasts?**
It handles talking-head video, tutorials, and screen recordings well, and many creators use it as their main video editor for those formats. What it is not built for is effects-heavy, cinematic, or colour-critical work; for that, a traditional desktop editor remains the better choice.

**How reliable is Overdub for fixing mistakes?**
For patching a single wrong word or a short phrase in your own cloned voice, it is impressively convincing. It is less suited to generating long passages of speech from scratch, where the seams become easier to hear, so treat it as a repair tool rather than a full narration engine.
