# Wordtune: A Good Rewriting Tool That Its Owner Stopped Developing

Wordtune is a genuinely good product in an uncomfortable position. It rewrites sentences better than most tools that do the same job, and reviewers who tested it liked the output. It is also, according to Israeli technology reporting, a product whose **development AI21 Labs discontinued in April 2025** as part of a retreat to enterprise business.

It is still on sale. You can subscribe today. That combination — actively billed, not actively built — is the thing every other review of this tool omits, and it should shape your decision more than any feature comparison.

## What is Wordtune?

Wordtune is an AI writing assistant built around sentence-level rewriting. You highlight a sentence, and it offers several alternative phrasings — more casual, more formal, shorter, expanded. Unlike tools that draft from a brief, Wordtune works on text you have already written.

That focus is its strength. The rewrite quality is high and the interface is unusually direct: no prompting, no chat, just a highlighted sentence and a list of alternatives. For non-native English writers it functions as a fluency layer over your own thinking, and for native speakers it breaks the loop of rereading a clumsy sentence without seeing the fix.

It also summarises documents, videos and PDFs, a capability that absorbed the separate **Wordtune Read** product when that was wound down.

It is owned by **AI21 Labs**, an Israeli AI company, and this is where the caution comes from.

## Key Features

- **Sentence rewriting** with multiple alternatives per sentence, the core feature and the best-executed one.
- **Tone adjustment** across casual and formal registers.
- **Shorten and expand** controls for length without a rewrite from scratch.
- **Summarising** of long documents, articles, PDFs and videos.
- **Browser extension** working across Gmail, Google Docs, LinkedIn and most web editors.
- **Microsoft Word integration** for people who draft in Word.
- **Multi-language input support** for writers working from another first language.

## Pros and Cons

**What is genuinely good**

The rewriting is excellent and remains the reason to consider it. Seeing four versions of your own sentence is a more useful editing experience than a single suggested correction, because you retain the judgement about which fits. Reviewers consistently rate output quality highly, and its Trustpilot score of roughly 4.1 is respectable — notably, complaints there concentrate on billing rather than on the product.

The interface deserves credit for restraint. There is no prompt to write and no conversation to manage, which for the specific job of improving a sentence is faster than asking a chatbot.

**The problem you are actually buying into**

AI21 has contracted severely. Reporting indicates the company **cut more than 60% of its workforce** by May 2026, going from roughly 180 employees to around 70, **halted development of its Jamba model family**, discontinued standalone model sales, and refocused on Maestro, its agent orchestration platform. A reported $300 million funding round never closed. Acquisition discussions with Nvidia in 2025 and with Nebius in 2026 both ended without a deal.

Against that backdrop, Wordtune is a consumer product at a company that has publicly retreated from consumer products. The practical risks are ordinary but real: features will not improve, competitors using newer models will pull ahead, support is likely thin, and the product could be withdrawn with limited notice. None of this makes today's rewrites worse. All of it argues against annual billing.

**Billing complaints are the dominant negative theme**

Reviews and complaint records describe a consistent pattern: cancellations that did not stop billing, users unable to locate a cancellation option, and charges at a tier the customer did not select — including one case of selecting a monthly plan and being charged an annual amount. One user reported roughly $360 charged over two years despite cancelling three times. Promotional trials converting at full price appear repeatedly. **The company's stated policy is that payments are non-refundable**, though in fairness a number of complainants did receive refunds after escalating publicly, and the company responds to negative reviews.

Support runs through in-app messaging with response times reported beyond a day.

## How to Use Wordtune

1. **Read the status section above before subscribing.** This is the unusual case where the company matters more than the feature list.
2. **Start with the free tier**, which allows a limited number of rewrites daily — commonly reported as around ten. That is enough to judge whether the rewriting suits your writing.
3. **Install the browser extension** rather than working on the website, since the value comes from rewriting where you already type.
4. **Write your draft badly and quickly first**, then rewrite. Wordtune is an editing tool, and trying to write well in the first pass wastes what it is good at.
5. **Compare alternatives rather than taking the first.** The list exists so you can exercise judgement; accepting the top option every time produces flat, uniform prose.
6. **Check each rewrite preserved your meaning.** Rewriters occasionally smooth a sentence into saying something slightly different, which is worse than an awkward original.
7. **Choose monthly billing if you subscribe.** Given both the billing complaints and the development status, an annual commitment to this particular product carries more risk than usual.

### FAQs

**Is Wordtune still available in 2026?**
Yes, the product is still available and still sold. However, reporting indicates AI21 Labs discontinued development in April 2025 as it refocused on enterprise. The accurate description is a product in maintenance rather than one that has shut down — available and billed, but not actively improved.

**Is it safe to subscribe to Wordtune?**
Your card will be charged and the product will work. The risks are that features stagnate, support is limited, and the product could eventually be withdrawn, given AI21 cut over 60% of staff and exited consumer-facing work. If you subscribe, monthly billing is the sensible choice, and cancel promptly if you stop using it given the documented billing friction.

**What does the free version of Wordtune allow?**
Reports consistently describe a daily cap on rewrites in the region of ten, plus a small monthly allowance of summaries, with unlimited basic spelling and grammar checking. Exact limits have changed over time, so confirm on the site — but the free tier is adequate for judging quality before paying.

**What happened to Wordtune Read?**
It was wound down as a standalone product, and its Chrome extension carried a retirement notice directing users to the main Wordtune extension. Document and video summarising survives inside the main product rather than as a separate tool.

**What are good alternatives to Wordtune?**
For rewriting specifically, QuillBot is the closest direct equivalent and is actively developed. Grammarly covers rewriting alongside broader checking. Any general assistant such as ChatGPT or Claude will rewrite sentences competently, though less quickly for single-sentence edits. Given the development status, an actively maintained alternative is the safer long-term commitment.
