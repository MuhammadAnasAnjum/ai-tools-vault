# AI Marketing and SEO Tools: What Each One Is Actually For

This is the category where ownership changes matter as much as features. In the last two years several of the biggest names here were acquired, rebranded or folded into larger suites, and a directory that still describes them the way they looked in 2023 will point you at the wrong product or the wrong price.

Grouping them by the job they do today — finding keywords, optimising content, generating ads, managing ad accounts, listening to your brand, or running AI across a CRM — is the clearest way through. Every review carries a verification date and flags where a price is second-hand.

## Search visibility and keyword research

**Semrush** is still the deepest all-in-one SEO and competitive-intelligence suite, and it changed hands: Adobe completed its acquisition on 28 April 2026, folding it into Adobe's customer-experience enterprise. The product works as before, and its newer push is AI Visibility — tracking whether your brand shows up inside AI answers. Read the review for what the Adobe ownership may mean for packaging, and treat the tier prices as second-hand.

## Optimising content for search

**Surfer SEO** is a content-optimisation specialist, not a full suite — its Content Editor scores drafts against what is already ranking. The review covers two things honestly: its Humanizer feature is positioned to evade AI detectors, and its Content Score correlates only weakly with rankings in independent testing, so treat it as a guide rather than a guarantee. Add-ons push the real cost well above the headline.

**MarketMuse** is built for content strategy at scale — surfacing gaps, clustering topics and generating briefs rather than drafting from a blank page. Siteimprove acquired it in late 2024, and the most practical consequence is that public pricing disappeared: it is now demo-and-quote only, so older reviews quoting a flat figure are describing a pricing model that no longer exists.

**AI SEO by Leap** is the budget entry here, pitched as a cheaper, faster alternative to an SEO agency. Two cautions run through its review: "Leap" is a badly overloaded name that maps to several unrelated products, and its live status could not be independently confirmed at the time of writing — so the piece tells you which Leap this is and advises confirming the tool is live before you subscribe.

## Generating ad creative and copy

**AdCreative.ai** generates ad visuals and copy scored by a conversion-prediction model, and is now an Appier company. Its review deals directly with a well-documented billing-complaint pattern — trial charges, a pricier tier sometimes pre-selected, and refund friction — so the practical advice is to read the trial terms, set a cancellation reminder, and start monthly.

**Pencil** is the cautionary tale of a self-serve tool that grew up: acquired by The Brandtech Group in 2023, it is now an enterprise "agentic marketing OS" orchestrating many AI models, sold by demo to large brands. If you arrived looking for the old small-business ad generator, it is effectively gone.

## Managing and optimising ad accounts

**AdCopy**, now developed as **Koast.ai** by Reign Technologies, is a Meta-only ad launching and automation platform — bulk-publishing variations across accounts with automated stop-loss pausing. Its review also untangles the three different products all called "AdCopy" so you land on the right one.

**Madgicx** is a Meta-only ads "super app" with AI targeting, creative analytics and a rules engine, aimed at e-commerce advertisers spending five figures a month. Pricing is spend-tiered rather than flat, and reviews are genuinely mixed — a strong feature set alongside recurring billing complaints — so the trial is the place to judge it.

**Plai** goes the other way from the Meta-only tools: it is cross-platform ad management built for small businesses and solo operators, spanning Meta, Google, TikTok, YouTube and more, with a daily "Optimize for Me" automation. It trades advanced depth for simplicity and a low entry price, which is exactly its audience.

## Listening to your brand

**Brand24** is AI social listening — real-time monitoring across millions of sources with sentiment analysis, spike alerts and a newer feature tracking your brand's visibility inside AI answers. The review is honest about the limits: sentiment tags are directional not definitive, and LinkedIn coverage is thin, which matters for B2B.

## Video and meetings

**vidIQ** is the YouTube growth toolkit — keyword research, competitor tracking and AI-assisted titles, tags and ideas. It is a useful research companion, but its review devotes real space to a substantiated billing-complaint pattern, so the guidance is to prefer monthly billing and diarise renewals.

**tl;dv** is an AI meeting recorder for Zoom, Meet and Teams, with a generous free tier and both a bot and a bot-free desktop option. There is no scandal here, but its review covers the category-level responsibility that comes with recording bots: get participant consent, since the legal duty sits with you, not the tool.

## AI across a CRM

**HubSpot AI**, now branded **Breeze**, is only as useful as the CRM it sits in — its whole advantage is acting on your own HubSpot data. The assistant is free across editions, while enrichment and autonomous agents are metered by credits. The review covers the July 2026 data-sharing episode plainly: a proposed default-on change that HubSpot reversed within days after customer backlash, and relevant context for anyone weighing data governance.

**Phrasee**, now trading as **Jacquard** after a 2024 rebrand, is enterprise-only AI for on-brand marketing language at scale — subject lines and campaign copy generated and optimised in a calibrated brand voice. There is no self-serve tier and no public price, and the review flags that the new name collides with an unrelated Google hardware project.

## Three patterns worth carrying into any purchase

**Ownership changes are the story in this category.** Semrush (Adobe), MarketMuse (Siteimprove), Pencil (Brandtech), AdCreative.ai (Appier) and Phrasee (rebranded Jacquard) have all changed hands or names recently. A comparison table written before 2025 is not slightly stale here — it may name the wrong company, the wrong product and the wrong price.

**Billing is where the complaints cluster, not output quality.** Across AdCreative.ai, vidIQ and Madgicx, the recurring theme in negative reviews is trials, renewals and refunds rather than the tools themselves. Start monthly wherever you can, read the trial and cancellation terms before entering a card, and diarise any refund deadline.

**Pricing is getting harder to see, not easier.** Several vendors here have moved to demo-only quotes, spend-tiered pricing or credit metering, and the frontier feature everyone is racing toward — tracking brand visibility inside AI answers — is usually a paid add-on. Confirm the current numbers on each vendor's own page before subscribing; every price in this category is flagged as second-hand for a reason.
