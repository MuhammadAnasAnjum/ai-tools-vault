# Midjourney in 2026: The Artist's Favourite, Under Legal Scrutiny

Few image generators inspire the loyalty Midjourney does. Ask a concept artist or an illustrator which tool produces the most striking results out of the box, and Midjourney's name tends to come up first. Its images carry a painterly, richly lit quality that competitors keep chasing and rarely match, and that reputation has built a community of millions around a comparatively small, self-funded research lab.

That reputation now sits alongside a serious legal question. Midjourney is defending itself against copyright claims from some of the largest studios in entertainment, and how that case resolves could shape the whole generative-image field. This midjourney review looks at what the tool does well, where it disappoints, the reported pricing, and the context around that litigation — without pretending the outcome is known.

## What is Midjourney?

Midjourney is an image-generation service built by Midjourney, Inc., an independent research lab founded in 2021 in San Francisco by David Holz, previously a co-founder of the motion-sensing company Leap Motion. The lab has stayed self-funded rather than taking large outside investment, and by reported figures it reached roughly $300 million in revenue in 2024 with more than 20 million users. That combination — small team, no venture pressure, large audience — is rare in the sector.

For a long time Midjourney ran exclusively through a Discord bot, which gave it a distinctive communal feel but also a steep learning curve. The web app at midjourney.com is now the primary interface and carries the full feature set, including gallery organisation, editing tools and account controls, while the original Discord bot remains supported for those who prefer it. The model has iterated quickly: version 7, released in April 2025, is the last firmly documented major release, and Midjourney updates its underlying model often enough that a newer version may well have appeared since.

## Key Features

- **High-aesthetic image generation** — the core draw, producing painterly, well-composed images with a signature look.
- **Web app interface** — a full-featured browser experience that has become the main way to create, organise and refine images.
- **Discord integration** — the original bot-based workflow, still supported for community-style prompting.
- **V1 Video model** — animates a still image into roughly five-second clips, extendable to about twenty-one seconds.
- **Style and reference controls** — tools for steering composition, style and consistency across generations.
- **Upscaling and variations** — refine a promising result into higher resolution or explore alternates.
- **Relaxed mode** — slower, non-priority generation that higher tiers receive in larger or unlimited amounts.
- **Active community gallery** — a large public feed that doubles as inspiration and a way to learn prompting.

## Pros and Cons

**What works well**

Image quality is the reason people stay. Midjourney's default output has an aesthetic polish — lighting, colour, composition — that tends to look finished rather than merely generated, which is why it remains a favourite for mood boards, concept art and editorial illustration. The move to a proper web app has softened the old barrier to entry, so newcomers no longer need to learn Discord commands to begin. The V1 Video model extends that strength into short motion clips, and the vast public gallery makes it easy to learn what prompts produce what results.

**Where it falls short**

The most immediate drawback is that there is no ongoing free tier. Occasional free trials appear and disappear, but you should assume a subscription is required to use the service, which raises the cost of simply evaluating it. Fine, precise control can also be harder than in some rivals — Midjourney optimises for beautiful output over pixel-exact direction, so users who need exact layouts or reliable in-image text sometimes reach for other tools. Video is early: clips are short and the feature is less mature than the still-image side. The larger uncertainty, though, is legal, and it deserves its own note.

**A note on the copyright litigation**

This is important context for anyone weighing a commercial commitment. In June 2025, Disney Enterprises and NBCUniversal (with DreamWorks named) sued Midjourney for copyright infringement in the Central District of California. Warner Bros. filed a separate suit, and the cases were consolidated in November 2025 with Disney as lead plaintiff. Midjourney has answered by asserting a fair-use defence. As of mid-2026 the matter is in discovery with no ruling on the merits — there has been no finding of liability, and the arguments on both sides remain unproven. It is worth following if your use is commercial, but nothing has been decided.

## How to Use Midjourney

1. **Create an account** — sign up through midjourney.com; a paid plan is generally required, since there is no standing free tier.
2. **Start in the web app** — the browser interface carries the full feature set and is the gentlest entry point for new users.
3. **Write a descriptive prompt** — describe subject, style, lighting and mood; Midjourney rewards evocative, specific language.
4. **Generate an initial grid** — review the set of options it returns rather than expecting a single perfect result.
5. **Refine with variations** — pick the closest candidate and generate variations to steer it toward what you want.
6. **Use style and reference controls** — apply reference images or style settings to keep a consistent look across a project.
7. **Upscale your favourite** — produce a higher-resolution version once you are happy with the composition.
8. **Try V1 Video** — animate a finished still into a short clip if you want motion, keeping the length limits in mind.

### FAQs

**Does Midjourney have a free plan?**
Not on an ongoing basis. Midjourney has run occasional free trials that come and go, but you should plan on a paid subscription to use it regularly. That makes it harder to evaluate cheaply than tools with a permanent free tier, so it helps to have a clear idea of what you want to produce before subscribing.

**How much does Midjourney cost?**
Reported plans run roughly from a Basic tier near $10 a month up through Standard, Pro and Mega tiers, with higher tiers adding more fast generation and relaxed-mode capacity. These figures are second-hand and change over time, so treat them as a rough guide and verify the current tiers on the vendor page before paying.

**What is the latest Midjourney version?**
Version 7, released in April 2025, is the last firmly verified major release. Midjourney updates its model rapidly, and newer versions may have appeared since, so check what the app currently offers rather than relying on a version number quoted in older guides.

**Is it safe to use Midjourney images commercially?**
There is genuine legal uncertainty because of the ongoing studio lawsuits, which remain unresolved and involve no finding of liability so far. For casual and personal work this is largely academic, but for commercial projects it is sensible to follow the case, read Midjourney's current terms, and take your own view on the risk.
