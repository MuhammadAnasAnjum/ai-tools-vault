# Taskade: Project Management That Became an AI App Builder

Taskade started as a clean, fast outliner for teams who found Notion bloated and Trello limiting. It still does that. But the product's centre of gravity has shifted decisively toward AI agents and, more recently, toward generating working software from a prompt.

Whether that shift is exciting or annoying depends entirely on what you came for — and long-time users are split on it.

## What is Taskade?

Taskade is a workspace that combines task lists, project outlines, mind maps, kanban boards, and documents in one structure, with AI agents layered on top. The underlying idea is that every project is a tree you can view six different ways without duplicating data.

The AI layer is where the product now invests. You build custom agents trained on your own uploaded documents and URLs, give them persistent memory of a workspace, then mention them inside a project to have them do work. Each agent can be assigned its own model, chosen from more than fifteen frontier options across the GPT, Claude, and Gemini families.

**Genesis** is the newest and most ambitious piece: describe what you want and it generates a working hosted web application, complete with database, interface, agents, and automations. Published apps run without consuming your credits, which makes the economics more reasonable than they first appear.

AI usage is metered in credits, from 10,000 monthly on Starter up to 1.1 million on Enterprise, with the free tier providing a one-time allocation of 3,000.

## Key Features

- **One project, six views** — switch between list, board, mind map, org chart, calendar, and table without restructuring anything.
- **Custom AI agents** — trained on your documents, with workspace memory, mentionable directly inside projects.
- **Per-agent model selection** — assign a cheap fast model to routine agents and a frontier model to the ones doing hard work.
- **Genesis app builder** — turns a prompt into a hosted application with its own database and UI.
- **Automations** — trigger-and-action workflows connecting Taskade to the tools around it.
- **Real-time collaboration** — shared editing, comments, and video chat built into the workspace.
- **Broad platform coverage** — web, iOS, Android, macOS, Windows, Linux, plus Chrome and Firefox extensions.
- **100+ integrations and an API**, though the MCP server is restricted to paid plans.

## Pros and Cons

**What makes it worth trying**

The multi-view model is genuinely well executed. Reorganising a project from an outline into a board takes one click and loses nothing, which is more than most competitors manage. Pricing is aggressive: Starter at $6/month annually undercuts Notion, Asana, and ClickUp meaningfully, and the free tier is usable for a solo user rather than being a demo. Platform coverage is unusually complete for a company this size, including Linux, which most rivals skip. And for building small internal tools — a tracker, a simple database app, a team dashboard — Genesis removes a real barrier for teams with no developer to spare.

**What to weigh**

The AI pivot has a cost, and long-time users name it directly: classic project management features have been sidelined while the app-builder story gets the attention. If you want a dependable task manager and nothing more, you may find the roadmap moving away from you.

The interface has grown cluttered as features accumulated, and onboarding confuses people — there is a lot of surface area and not much guidance through it. Performance degrades on large projects, with reported lag and stutter. Credit limits on the lower paid tiers are restrictive enough that heavy AI use pushes you upmarket faster than the headline price suggests. And agents misroute when their instructions are loose, so getting reliable behaviour takes more prompt discipline than the marketing implies.

Pricing runs, billed annually: Free $0, Starter $6/month, Pro $16/month, Business $40/month, Max $200/month, and Enterprise around $400/month, with monthly billing considerably higher. Free tier seat counts are reported inconsistently as one or two users, so verify before planning around it.

## How to Use Taskade

1. **Sign up free at taskade.com** and create a workspace before installing anything — the web app is the full product.
2. **Build one real project first.** Import an actual list of work rather than exploring templates, then try switching views on it. That is the fastest way to judge whether the model suits you.
3. **Learn the view switching.** Outline for thinking, board for status, calendar for deadlines. The same data, and no migration between them.
4. **Create one agent with a narrow job.** Train it on specific documents and give it tight instructions. Vague agents produce vague results, which is the most common disappointment.
5. **Match models to jobs.** Put a cheap fast model behind routine agents and reserve frontier models for the ones doing analysis. This directly controls your credit burn.
6. **Try Genesis on a small internal tool** — a request tracker or simple inventory list — rather than something business-critical on the first attempt.
7. **Watch your credit consumption in the first month** before committing annually, since the lower tiers run out faster than expected.

### FAQs

**How much does Taskade cost?**
There is a free plan with a one-time allocation of 3,000 AI credits. Paid tiers billed annually run roughly $6/month for Starter, $16 for Pro, $40 for Business, $200 for Max, and around $400 for Enterprise, with monthly billing priced considerably higher. Each tier includes a monthly AI credit allowance ranging from 10,000 to 1.1 million. No student plan was found. Confirm current figures on Taskade's plan page.

**Is Taskade still good for plain project management?**
Yes, the multi-view outliner remains capable and fast. The honest caveat is that development attention has shifted toward AI agents and the Genesis app builder, and long-standing users have noticed core project management features receiving less investment. If you want a tool whose roadmap is focused purely on task management, that is worth weighing.

**What is Taskade Genesis?**
It generates a working, hosted web application from a text description, including database, interface, AI agents, and automations. It targets internal tools — trackers, simple databases, team dashboards — rather than production software. Published apps run without consuming your AI credits, which makes it more practical than metered alternatives.

**How does Taskade compare to Notion?**
Taskade is faster, cheaper, and better at switching between project views. Notion is far more flexible as a database and has a much larger template and integration ecosystem. Teams wanting a structured task tool with AI agents usually prefer Taskade; teams building a customised knowledge base usually prefer Notion.
