# Microsoft Copilot: What Bing Chat Became, and Whether It Earns Its Place

Copilot has been renamed more often than most products get updated. It launched as Bing Chat, became Copilot, sprouted a $20 Copilot Pro tier, then absorbed that tier into Microsoft 365 subscriptions. If you have lost track of what Copilot is and what it costs, that is a reasonable reaction to a genuinely confusing few years.

Here is the current picture, and the honest answer about who should care.

## What is Microsoft Copilot?

Microsoft Copilot is an AI assistant woven through Microsoft's products — Windows, Edge, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, and a standalone app on the web, desktop, and mobile. It answers questions, searches the web, generates images, drafts and edits documents, and increasingly takes actions rather than just producing text.

The pricing structure changed materially. **Copilot Pro, the standalone $20/month consumer tier, was discontinued in October 2025, with support ending on 1 August 2026.** Consumer Copilot now arrives bundled: Microsoft 365 Personal at $9.99/month and Family at $12.99/month include it, and Microsoft 365 Premium, launched in October 2025 at $199.99/year list price, adds the highest usage limits along with the Researcher and Analyst agents.

Underneath, Copilot runs OpenAI's GPT-5 family, with GPT-5.2 selectable inside Microsoft 365 Copilot. Consumer Copilot has no manual model picker — a "Smart" mode routes automatically. Microsoft also runs its own in-house MAI models for voice, vision, image generation, and transcription features.

## Key Features

- **Native Office integration** — the real differentiator. Copilot drafts in Word, builds formulas and analyses data in Excel, generates slides in PowerPoint, and summarises threads in Outlook.
- **Copilot Vision** — shares what is on your screen or camera so you can ask about what you are looking at.
- **Voice mode** — spoken conversation, with a wake word on Windows.
- **Think Deeper and Deep Research** — extended reasoning for hard questions and long multi-source research reports.
- **Pages** — a persistent canvas where AI output and your own edits live side by side, instead of scrolling chat history.
- **Researcher and Analyst agents** — specialised agents on higher tiers for structured research and data analysis.
- **Memory and connectors** — recalls your preferences and pulls context from your Microsoft account and connected services.
- **Windows and Edge integration** — built into the operating system and browser, including agentic Edge Actions that complete tasks on web pages.

## Pros and Cons

**Its genuine strengths**

If your work lives in Office, no competitor comes close on integration. Copilot in Excel can see your actual spreadsheet; ChatGPT sees whatever you paste. That difference compounds across Word, Outlook, and PowerPoint. The economics are also better than they look: if you already pay for Microsoft 365, Copilot is included at no additional cost, making it the cheapest capable assistant most office workers can access. Copilot Vision is a well-executed feature, and the free tier is perfectly serviceable for everyday questions and drafting.

**Its real problems**

Microsoft's aggressive integration of Copilot into Windows generated substantial user backlash — enough that the company reversed course in 2025, reducing Copilot's presence in Photos, Notepad, Widgets, and the Snipping Tool. The Recall feature triggered a separate privacy uproar. The naming and pricing churn has left users unsure what they are buying, and the retirement of Copilot Pro annoyed people who had standardised on it. Microsoft does not publish exact free-tier limits, so you discover them by hitting them. And because consumer Copilot hides model selection, you have less control over how a query is handled than on ChatGPT or Claude. For pure conversational quality and flexibility, it trails both.

## How to Use Microsoft Copilot

1. **Open copilot.microsoft.com** or use the Copilot app on Windows, macOS, iOS, or Android. A free Microsoft account is enough to start.
2. **Sign in with the account that holds your files.** Copilot's advantage is context, and it can only use documents in the account you are signed into.
3. **Work inside the Office app, not the chat window,** when a document is involved. Use the Copilot button in Word or Excel so it can act on the file directly.
4. **Try Vision for anything visual.** Share your screen and ask about a chart, an error message, or a form rather than describing it in words.
5. **Switch on Think Deeper for hard problems.** The default mode optimises for speed, which is the wrong trade-off for analysis.
6. **Use Pages for anything you will keep.** Chat history is a poor container for work you intend to reuse or refine.
7. **Check the output against the source.** Copilot summarising your own documents is reliable more often than Copilot summarising the web, but neither is exempt from verification.

### FAQs

**What happened to Copilot Pro?**
Microsoft discontinued it in October 2025 and ended support on 1 August 2026. Its features moved into Microsoft 365 consumer subscriptions. If you want the highest usage limits and the Researcher and Analyst agents, Microsoft 365 Premium is the current equivalent. Existing Pro subscribers were migrated rather than cut off.

**Do I need Microsoft 365 to use Copilot?**
No. There is a free tier on the web, in Windows, and in the mobile apps that handles questions, drafting, image generation, and web search. What a Microsoft 365 subscription adds is Copilot working *inside* Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook on your actual files, plus higher usage limits. That in-app capability is the reason most people pay.

**Is Copilot the same as ChatGPT?**
They share underlying OpenAI models, so raw answer quality is similar. The products differ in everything else. Copilot is optimised for Microsoft's ecosystem and hides model selection behind automatic routing; ChatGPT offers more control, more features, and a larger third-party ecosystem. Choose based on where your work already lives.

**Is Copilot free version good enough for students?**
For most coursework, yes — drafting, explaining concepts, summarising readings, and generating images are all in the free tier. Students with a Microsoft 365 subscription through their institution should check whether Copilot is already included, since many education licences now bundle it.
