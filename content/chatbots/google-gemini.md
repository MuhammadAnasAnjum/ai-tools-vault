# Google Gemini: Deep Google Integration, With Some Rough Edges

Gemini began life as Bard, a rushed answer to ChatGPT that Google visibly regretted. Several generations later it is a different proposition: a capable assistant whose real advantage is that it sits inside Gmail, Docs, Drive, and Chrome, where a great deal of work already happens.

The models are strong. The pricing and usage rules are where Gemini asks for patience.

## What is Google Gemini?

Gemini is Google's family of AI models and the assistant built on them. You use it at gemini.google.com, in dedicated Android, iOS, and macOS apps, inside Google Workspace apps, and through Chrome integration.

The current lineup is **Gemini 3.1 Pro** as the flagship, **Gemini 3.6 Flash** as the fast everyday model, and **Gemini 3.1 Deep Think** for the hardest reasoning problems, restricted to the top subscription tier and to adult accounts.

Consumer access is sold through Google's AI plans rather than as a standalone Gemini subscription. There is a free tier, **Google AI Pro at $19.99/month**, and **Google AI Ultra starting at $99.99/month** with a higher top tier above it. Ultra's pricing has been revised more than once, including at I/O 2026, so check the official plans page rather than trusting a comparison article. For businesses, Google retired the standalone Workspace AI add-on and now bundles AI capability into Business and Enterprise plans.

## Key Features

- **Workspace integration** — drafts in Gmail, writes in Docs, builds in Sheets, and pulls from Drive without you uploading anything.
- **Deep Research** — generates long cited reports, and can use your own Gmail, Drive, and NotebookLM content as sources alongside the web.
- **Gems** — custom assistants with saved instructions for tasks you repeat, similar in spirit to custom GPTs.
- **Canvas** — a side-by-side editing surface for documents and code, better suited to iteration than a chat transcript.
- **Audio Overviews** — turns documents or research into a conversational audio summary, useful for commutes.
- **Veo video generation** — text-to-video on paid tiers, one of the more capable offerings in a consumer assistant.
- **Gemini Agent** — multi-step task completion, currently limited to Ultra subscribers on the web in the US.
- **Personalisation and memory** — recalls context across sessions, optionally informed by your Google activity.

## Pros and Cons

**Where Gemini wins**

Context, without the copy-and-paste. Asking Gemini to summarise a thread in your inbox or pull figures from a Drive spreadsheet works because it already has access. Deep Research is excellent, and being able to point it at your own Gmail and Drive as sources is something no competitor matches cleanly. The free tier is capable rather than crippled, Veo makes paid plans genuinely differentiated, and the multimodal handling of images, audio, and long documents is first-rate. If your organisation runs on Workspace, Gemini has a structural advantage that no amount of ChatGPT features closes.

**Where it disappoints**

Gemini moved to compute-based metering rather than published prompt counts, refreshing every five hours under a weekly cap. The intent is fairness; the effect is that you cannot easily predict when you will run out, and heavy media generation or Deep Research burns through allowance quickly. When you overflow, you are downgraded to a lighter model. That change drew real criticism. Google's safety tuning also produces over-refusal — the 2024 suspension of people-image generation was the high-profile case, and users reported filter regressions again in 2026, where ordinary requests get declined. Feature availability is heavily region-gated, with several of the most interesting capabilities US-only at launch. And the model naming is a mess: versions arrive, get announced, and sometimes ship on unclear timelines, which makes it hard to know what you are actually talking to.

## How to Use Google Gemini

1. **Go to gemini.google.com** signed into the Google account that holds your mail and files, or install the app. The free tier needs no payment details.
2. **Reference your own content directly.** Ask it to find the invoice in your Drive or summarise last week's thread — this is the capability worth paying for.
3. **Pick the right model for the task.** Flash for quick drafting and rewriting, Pro for analysis and long documents, Deep Think for genuinely hard reasoning if your plan includes it.
4. **Use Deep Research for anything substantial.** Give it a scoped question and let it run for several minutes rather than asking a one-line query.
5. **Build a Gem for repeated work.** Encode the instructions once and stop re-briefing it every session.
6. **Move to Canvas when you start editing.** Iterating on a document in chat is far clumsier than working on it in place.
7. **Watch your compute budget.** Video and image generation consume disproportionately. If you have real work planned, do it before you experiment with Veo.

### FAQs

**Is Google Gemini free?**
Yes, there is a free tier with access to the everyday models and a share of the more capable ones. Google AI Pro at $19.99/month raises limits and unlocks more features, and Google AI Ultra starts at $99.99/month for the highest limits, Deep Think, and Gemini Agent. Because Google has repriced Ultra more than once, confirm the current structure on the official plans page.

**How many prompts do I get per day?**
Google no longer publishes a simple daily prompt count. Usage is metered by compute consumed, refreshing every five hours within a weekly cap, and different activities cost different amounts — media generation and Deep Research consume far more than a text question. Older articles quoting specific daily figures describe a system that has been replaced.

**Is Gemini better than ChatGPT?**
For anyone working inside Gmail, Docs, and Drive, Gemini's access to your own content is a decisive practical advantage. ChatGPT has the broader feature set, larger ecosystem, and more predictable usage limits. If you use Google Workspace daily, start with Gemini; otherwise ChatGPT is the safer default.

**Why does Gemini refuse reasonable requests?**
Google's safety tuning is conservative, and it periodically over-corrects — users have reported ordinary requests being declined after filter changes. Rephrasing with clear context about why you need the information often works. If a specific category is consistently blocked, another assistant may simply be the better tool for that task.
