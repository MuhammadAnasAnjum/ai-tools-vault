# Phrasee Review 2026: The Enterprise Copy AI Now Called Jacquard

If you have been researching Phrasee and landed on a company called Jacquard, you are in the right place. Phrasee officially rebranded to Jacquard on 12 June 2024. It is the same company and the same product, simply under a new name, so everything written here about Phrasee applies to Jacquard today.

Phrasee, now Jacquard, is an enterprise platform that uses AI to generate on-brand marketing language at scale. It is not a self-serve tool you sign up for on a card; it is a custom-quoted product aimed at large organisations. This review explains what it does, who it is for, and how to evaluate it.

## What is Phrasee (Jacquard)?

Phrasee began in 2015 as a London-based specialist in optimising email subject lines with AI, testing and learning which wording drove the best engagement. Over the years it broadened well beyond subject lines into full-lifecycle campaign copy, generating and optimising language across email, web, app and push notifications.

Today, as Jacquard, the platform is positioned as enterprise "agentic" AI for marketing language. Its distinctive feature is calibrated brand-voice models: rather than generic output, it produces copy tuned to a specific brand's tone and guidelines, and it works across 17 or more languages. The pitch is consistent, on-brand messaging produced far faster than a human team could manage alone.

The company cites enterprise clients including TUI, Walgreens and Farfetch, and makes performance claims such as its AI-generated language beating human-written controls. Those performance figures are vendor-stated, so treat them as marketing claims to validate in a pilot rather than independently verified facts. Jacquard is now majority-owned by a private-equity investor group led by Capital D.

## Pros and Cons

**What works well**

For large organisations, the core value proposition is genuinely compelling: consistent, on-brand copy produced at a scale and speed no human team can match, and across many languages at once. The brand-voice calibration is the key differentiator, because it addresses the main worry about AI-written marketing copy, that it will sound generic or off-brand. A well-tuned model keeps the voice recognisably yours.

The company's heritage in performance optimisation also matters. This is not a general text generator repurposed for marketing; it grew up testing which wording actually drives engagement, and that discipline is baked into the product. For enterprises running high-volume, multi-channel campaigns, that focus on measurable outcomes is a real strength.

**Where it falls short**

The obvious limitation is accessibility. Phrasee, now Jacquard, is enterprise-only with custom pricing, so there are no public self-serve tiers and no verified starting price. Small and mid-sized businesses are effectively out of scope, and even large buyers have to go through a sales process to learn what it costs. That opacity makes quick comparison impossible.

Buyers should also keep performance claims in perspective. Statements such as the AI beating human-written controls are vendor-stated, and while plausible, they should be validated on your own campaigns during a pilot rather than accepted at face value. As with any enterprise martech purchase, the value depends heavily on volume, integration and how well the brand-voice model is calibrated to your needs.

**The rebrand and a naming note, for context**

To be clear once more: Phrasee became Jacquard on 12 June 2024. It is the same company, founded in 2015 and based in London, with the same product, now under private-equity ownership led by Capital D. If you know it as Phrasee, that history is intact; only the name has changed.
