# Chatbase: Turning Your Help Docs Into an AI Support Agent

Chatbase solves a different problem from the assistants it sits alongside in this category. You are not the user — your customers are. You point it at your documentation, it builds a chatbot that answers questions from that material, and you embed the result on your site.

It is one of the more commercially successful products in this space, and it is bootstrapped: founder and CEO Yasser Elsaid owns it outright with no outside investors, and the company passed $10 million in annual recurring revenue in mid-2026 with more than 8,000 paying customers. That matters for a tool you would build a support workflow on.

## What is Chatbase?

Chatbase is a no-code platform for building AI support agents trained on your own content. You upload documents, point it at your website, or connect a knowledge base; it ingests that material and creates an agent that answers customer questions using it rather than from general knowledge.

The company claims agents can resolve around 80% of incoming tickets, with the remainder escalated to humans. Beyond answering, agents take **AI Actions** — capturing leads, booking through Calendly or Cal.com, processing Stripe operations, searching the web, or posting into Slack — so the bot can complete a request instead of just describing how.

You choose the underlying model per agent, drawing from OpenAI and Anthropic families, and that choice directly affects cost. Anthropic has published Chatbase as a customer case study, and Claude models are a documented part of its backbone.

## Key Features

- **Train on your own sources** — documents, website crawls, help centres, and plain text, so answers reflect your product rather than the internet's guess at it.
- **Per-agent model selection** — pick a cheap fast model for simple FAQ deflection or a stronger one for nuanced technical support.
- **AI Actions** — lead capture, scheduling, web search, Stripe operations, and Slack notifications triggered from inside a conversation.
- **Multi-channel deployment** — embeddable web widget plus WhatsApp, Slack, Instagram, and Facebook Messenger.
- **Human handoff** — escalate to a person when the agent cannot resolve a query, rather than looping the customer.
- **Analytics** — see what customers actually ask, which is often the most valuable output for a product team.
- **API access** — integrate the agent into your own application rather than using the supplied widget.
- **Custom branding** — match the widget to your site on paid tiers.

## Pros and Cons

**Why teams choose it**

Setup is genuinely fast. Pointing it at existing documentation and having a working agent the same afternoon is realistic, which is not true of most support automation. Grounding answers in your own content sharply reduces the invented-answer problem that makes general chatbots unusable for customer-facing support. The channel coverage is broad for the price, and the analytics quietly reveal gaps in your documentation you did not know existed. For a small team without engineering capacity to spare, it removes a real barrier.

**Where it gets expensive and awkward**

The credit system is the dominant complaint, and it deserves scrutiny. Replies consume between one and six credits depending on the model — a light model costs roughly one, a top-tier reasoning model around five, and voice runs about six credits per minute. The practical result is that identical ticket volumes can cost wildly different amounts: one published estimate put 10,000 tickets anywhere between $600 and $4,600 depending purely on model choice. Budget by modelling your actual volume, not by reading the headline plan price.

Beyond cost, every tier including the most expensive standard plan includes only a single AI agent, so running separate bots for separate products means paying more. Free-tier agents are deleted after 14 days of inactivity, and free training data is capped at 400KB. Users also report hallucinations and drift in answer quality, which means an agent needs monitoring after launch rather than being set and forgotten.

Pricing itself is worth verifying directly: reported tiers cluster around a free plan, roughly $32/month for the entry paid tier, about $120/month mid-range, and around $400/month for the multi-seat tier, with annual billing discounted 20% and custom enterprise pricing above that. Third-party sources quote conflicting entry prices, so check the official page.

## How to Use Chatbase

1. **Sign up at chatbase.co** and create your first agent on the free tier before committing to a plan.
2. **Feed it your best documentation.** Quality of sources determines quality of answers more than any other factor — clean up your help docs first if they are thin or contradictory.
3. **Choose a model deliberately.** Start with a cheap fast model and only upgrade if answer quality genuinely requires it. This single decision drives most of your bill.
4. **Test with your real questions.** Pull the twenty most common tickets from your inbox and run them through the agent before it goes live.
5. **Write clear escalation rules.** Decide what the agent should never attempt to answer — billing disputes, security issues, anything legally sensitive — and route those to a human.
6. **Embed the widget** on your site, or connect WhatsApp, Slack, or Messenger depending on where your customers actually reach you.
7. **Review analytics weekly.** Look for questions the agent answered badly, then fix the underlying documentation rather than patching the prompt.

### FAQs

**How much does Chatbase actually cost per month?**
The plan fee is only part of it. Each tier includes a credit allowance, and replies consume one to six credits depending on which model handles them, with voice consuming around six per minute. Two businesses on the same plan can see very different effective costs. Estimate your monthly conversation volume, multiply by the credit cost of your chosen model, and compare that against the allowance before subscribing.

**Will the chatbot make things up about my product?**
It is much less likely to than a general-purpose chatbot, because answers are grounded in your uploaded content. Less likely is not never — users do report hallucinations. Keep your source material accurate and non-contradictory, test edge cases before launch, and review transcripts regularly rather than assuming the agent is behaving.

**Do I need any coding skills?**
No. Building and training an agent is entirely no-code, and embedding it is a copy-paste snippet. An API exists for teams that want deeper integration, but the standard path requires no development work.

**How does Chatbase compare to a traditional helpdesk?**
It is not a replacement for a ticketing system — it sits in front of one. Chatbase deflects repetitive questions so your team handles the cases that need judgement. Most teams run it alongside their existing helpdesk, with escalation wired between them.
