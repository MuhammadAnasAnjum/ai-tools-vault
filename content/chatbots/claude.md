# Claude AI: A Clear-Eyed Look at Anthropic's Assistant in 2026

Claude has built its reputation on a narrower promise than its rivals: fewer flashy consumer features, more reliability on long, difficult, text-heavy work. Writers, lawyers, analysts, and developers tend to be the people who switch to Claude and stay.

A note on naming, because it causes real confusion. Claude 3.5 is retired. The current generation is **Claude Opus 5** and **Claude Sonnet 5**, and older guides referencing 3.5 Sonnet or Claude 2 describe models you can no longer use. This review reflects the lineup as of August 2026.

## What is Claude?

Claude is a family of AI assistants from Anthropic, an AI safety company founded by former OpenAI researchers. You reach it through a chat interface at claude.ai, desktop and mobile apps, a command-line tool for developers, and an API.

Three model tiers do different jobs. **Sonnet 5** is the everyday default on Free and Pro plans — quick, capable, and the right choice for most writing and analysis. **Opus 5** is the heavier reasoning model for genuinely hard problems, and it draws from a separate, smaller usage allowance. **Fable 5** sits at the top capability tier, though availability there has shifted more than once in recent months.

What distinguishes Claude in practice is less the benchmark scores than the working style. It handles very long inputs without losing the thread, follows detailed instructions closely, pushes back rather than agreeing reflexively, and writes prose that needs less editing than most competitors produce.

## Key Features

- **Cowork** — agentic sessions where Claude works through multi-step tasks against your actual files, available on desktop for paid plans and in beta elsewhere.
- **Agent Skills** — reusable instruction packages that teach Claude a repeatable workflow. Anthropic published the format as an open spec, so skills are portable.
- **Claude Code** — a terminal-based coding agent included with every paid plan, capable of navigating and editing whole repositories.
- **Large context handling** — paste an entire contract, transcript, or codebase and ask questions across all of it without the model losing earlier detail.
- **Connectors and MCP** — links to external tools and data sources through the Model Context Protocol, an open standard Anthropic created and other vendors have adopted.
- **Memory** — carries relevant context between conversations, with a newer implementation on Free, Pro, and Max plans.
- **Office add-ins** — works directly inside Excel, PowerPoint, Word, and Outlook.
- **Artifacts** — renders documents, code, and interactive components in a side panel you can iterate on rather than copying out of chat.

## Pros and Cons

**Where Claude excels**

Long-document work is the clearest win. Feed it a 200-page report and its answers stay anchored to the text instead of drifting into generic summary. Writing quality is the second — Claude's default register is less padded and less list-obsessed than most assistants, which means less cleanup. For developers, Claude Code being bundled into every paid plan rather than sold separately is unusually good value. And Anthropic's safety posture translates into practical benefits: the model is more willing to say it does not know, and more likely to flag a flaw in your reasoning than to flatter it.

**Where it frustrates**

Usage limits are Claude's genuine sore point, and they are worth understanding before you commit. Rather than a message count, every plan runs on a rolling five-hour session window, with paid plans adding weekly caps and Opus drawing on its own separate bucket. Because usage pools across web, desktop, mobile, and Claude Code, a heavy coding session can consume the allowance you were saving for writing. Anthropic throttled subscriptions in March 2026 to manage capacity, prompting significant user backlash, and staff have confirmed that peak weekday hours drain sessions faster. The company was sued in June 2026 over how the Max plan's limits were advertised. Separately, the consumer feature set is thinner than ChatGPT's — no image generation, a smaller integration ecosystem, and fewer casual-use extras.

## How to Use Claude

1. **Sign up at claude.ai** with an email address, or install the desktop or mobile app. The free plan requires no payment details.
2. **Lead with your source material.** Claude's strength is reading — attach the document, spreadsheet, or code before asking your question rather than describing the contents.
3. **Be explicit about the output you want.** Specify audience, tone, length, and structure. Claude follows detailed instructions unusually literally, which is a feature once you lean into it.
4. **Stay on Sonnet for routine work.** Save Opus for genuinely hard reasoning, since its allowance is separate and smaller. Burning Opus on email drafts is a common mistake.
5. **Use Projects for recurring context.** Standing instructions and a shared file set stop you re-explaining your situation every session.
6. **Write a Skill for anything you repeat.** If you brief Claude the same way weekly, encode it once and invoke it thereafter.
7. **Watch your session window.** Heavy work early in a five-hour block leaves less for later. Front-load the tasks that matter most.

### FAQs

**What happened to Claude 3.5 Sonnet?**
It was retired as newer generations shipped. Anthropic's current models are Sonnet 5, Opus 5, and Fable 5. If a tutorial or comparison mentions Claude 3.5, 3.7, or Claude 2, it is describing a model that is no longer available, and its benchmark claims no longer apply.

**How much does Claude cost?**
There is a free plan. Pro is $20/month or $200/year. Max comes in two higher-usage tiers at $100 and $200/month, billed monthly only. Team plans are per seat with a two-member minimum, and Enterprise is annual and quoted directly. Paid plans can also purchase additional usage credits. Verify current pricing on Anthropic's site before subscribing, since the tiers have changed more than once.

**Why do I hit limits so quickly on Claude?**
Because the limit is a rolling time window, not a message count, and it is shared across every surface you use Claude on. Long attachments, big codebases, and Claude Code sessions consume it fastest. Using Sonnet instead of Opus for routine tasks, starting fresh conversations rather than extending enormous ones, and avoiding peak weekday mornings in US Pacific hours all help meaningfully.

**Claude or ChatGPT — which should I pick?**
Choose Claude if your work is reading and writing long documents, careful analysis, or coding, and you value output that needs less editing. Choose ChatGPT if you want the broadest feature set, image generation, and the largest integration ecosystem. At $20/month each, the honest answer for heavy users is that they solve different problems well enough to justify both.
