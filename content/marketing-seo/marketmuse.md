# MarketMuse Review 2026: AI Content Strategy, Now Part of Siteimprove

MarketMuse is a content intelligence platform built for planning rather than drafting. Instead of writing articles for you, it analyses large volumes of content to tell you what to write about, where your coverage is thin, and how to structure topics so they actually build authority.

That strategic focus sets it apart from the many tools that simply generate text. This review looks at what MarketMuse does well, who it suits, and two developments that shape any 2026 decision: its acquisition by Siteimprove and the fact that its pricing is no longer publicly listed.

## What is MarketMuse?

MarketMuse is an AI-powered content strategy and intelligence tool. Its core job is to analyse content at scale — yours and your competitors' — and surface gaps and opportunities. It uses topic modelling to map how comprehensively a subject is covered, identifies where you could rank with more or better content, and helps plan content clusters that reinforce one another.

A practical output is the AI-generated content brief. Rather than handing a writer a bare keyword, MarketMuse produces a structured brief with subtopics, questions and related terms to cover, giving writers a research head start. This is squarely a planning tool, aimed at deciding what to create and how to frame it, not at drafting from a blank page.

That distinction matters when choosing tools. If you want software to write paragraphs, MarketMuse is not it. If you want to make smarter decisions about which topics to pursue and how to structure a content library so it builds topical authority, that is exactly the problem MarketMuse is designed to solve.

## Key Features

- **Content gap analysis** — identifies topics and subtopics you are missing compared with what ranks.
- **Topic modelling** — maps how thoroughly a subject is covered and where depth is lacking.
- **Content cluster analysis** — helps plan groups of related pages that reinforce topical authority.
- **AI-generated content briefs** — structured briefs with subtopics, questions and terms for writers to follow.
- **Competitive content analysis** — compares your coverage of a topic against competing pages.
- **Content inventory and auditing** — assesses existing content at scale to find what to update or consolidate.
- **Personalised difficulty scoring** — estimates how hard it would be for your specific site to rank for a topic.
- **Content planning workflows** — turns analysis into prioritised, actionable plans for a content team.

## Pros and Cons

**What works well**

MarketMuse is strong where most content tools are weak: strategy at scale. For teams managing large content libraries, its ability to analyse coverage and pinpoint gaps is genuinely useful, replacing guesswork about what to write next with data-led prioritisation. The personalised difficulty scoring is a thoughtful touch, since it judges opportunities relative to your own site's authority rather than a generic benchmark.

The content briefs are another practical win. Handing a writer a well-researched brief, complete with subtopics and questions to answer, raises the floor on quality and saves hours of upfront research. For content operations that run at volume, the planning discipline MarketMuse encourages can pay for itself in fewer wasted articles.

**Where it falls short**

The clearest drawback in 2026 is reduced pricing transparency. Public pricing was removed after the acquisition, so you now have to request a demo and a quote to find out what it costs. That opacity makes it harder to evaluate quickly, especially for smaller teams weighing it against tools with clear, self-serve prices.

There is also strategic uncertainty. As part of a larger suite now, MarketMuse's long-term standalone roadmap is less certain than it was, and buyers making a longer commitment should factor that in. The tool is also aimed at strategy rather than writing, so it is not a fit if you want something to draft content for you.

**The Siteimprove acquisition, for context**

Worth stating plainly: Siteimprove announced its acquisition of MarketMuse on 8 October 2024 and completed it for an undisclosed sum. The product now operates as "MarketMuse by Siteimprove." Importantly, it is still a standalone product as of mid-2026 and has not been sunset, but it is being folded into Siteimprove's broader SEO, accessibility and analytics suite. The main practical effect so far is the shift to demo-and-quote pricing. The open question is how independent the product remains over the longer term as integration deepens, which is the kind of thing worth asking about directly on a sales call before committing.

## How to Use MarketMuse

1. **Start with the free tier** — a limited free account, reportedly around ten queries a month, lets you sample the analysis before talking to sales.
2. **Define your topic** — enter the subject you want to build authority in, so MarketMuse can model how it is covered across the web.
3. **Run a content inventory** — let it audit your existing pages to see what you already cover and where the gaps sit.
4. **Review the gap analysis** — identify the subtopics and questions competitors address that you do not, and prioritise the ones that fit your site.
5. **Check personalised difficulty** — use the difficulty scoring to focus on topics you can realistically rank for given your current authority.
6. **Generate content briefs** — create structured briefs for the priority topics and hand them to your writers as a research foundation.
7. **Plan clusters, not one-offs** — group related briefs into clusters so the pages reinforce one another rather than competing.
8. **Request a demo for pricing** — since public pricing was removed, book a demo to get a current quote and ask about the standalone roadmap.

### FAQs

**Is MarketMuse still available after the Siteimprove acquisition?**
Yes. Siteimprove completed its acquisition, but MarketMuse still operates as a standalone product, now branded "MarketMuse by Siteimprove," and has not been sunset as of mid-2026. It is being integrated into Siteimprove's wider suite over time.

**How much does MarketMuse cost?**
Public pricing was removed after the acquisition, so pricing is now demo-and-quote based. A limited free tier reportedly persists. Some third-party sources cite paid tiers from around $99 a month, but this is unconfirmed and should not be treated as authoritative, so request a current quote from the vendor.

**Does MarketMuse write content for me?**
Not primarily. Its focus is content strategy and intelligence: gap analysis, topic modelling and content briefs. It helps you decide what to write and how to structure it, then hands writers a detailed brief. It is best paired with a separate drafting tool or human writers.

**Who is MarketMuse best for?**
Teams and businesses managing sizeable content libraries who need to plan strategically and build topical authority. Solo bloggers or those who mainly want an article generator may find it more than they need, especially given the shift to quote-only pricing.
