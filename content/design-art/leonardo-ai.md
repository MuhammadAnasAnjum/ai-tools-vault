# Leonardo AI: Fine-Grained Control Under Canva's Wing in 2026

Where some image generators aim to hand you a finished picture from a single prompt, Leonardo AI has always leaned the other way — toward giving you levers. Real-time canvases, inpainting, reference controls and a deep catalogue of fine-tuned models make it a favourite among people who want to shape an image rather than accept whatever the first generation offers. That control-first philosophy is its defining trait.

The other defining fact is corporate: Leonardo is now part of Canva, though it continues to run as its own product. This leonardo ai review explains what the tool does, how the Canva relationship works, what its Phoenix model brought, and the reported pricing — while clearing up a couple of naming mix-ups that regularly trip people up.

## What is Leonardo AI?

Leonardo.Ai is a Sydney-based image-generation platform founded in December 2022, led by chief executive JJ Fiasson. In July 2024 Canva announced its acquisition of the company, in a deal reported above US$320 million, but Leonardo has continued to operate as a standalone product and brand rather than being folded away. It claims a user base above twelve million, and it sits in an interesting position: independent enough to keep its own identity, yet backed by one of the design world's biggest names.

The technical turning point was Phoenix, Leonardo's first in-house foundational model, launched in June 2024 with notably better prompt adherence and the ability to render legible text within images. Phoenix runs alongside Leonardo's own Kino XL model and a set of integrated third-party models, and its influence reaches beyond Leonardo itself — Canva's own Dream Lab generator is built on the Phoenix architecture. That shared foundation is a useful signal of how central Leonardo's technology has become inside its parent company.

## Key Features

- **Phoenix model** — Leonardo's in-house foundation model, with strong prompt adherence and legible in-image text.
- **Real-time AI canvas** — an interactive surface where edits and generations update live as you work.
- **Inpainting and outpainting** — repaint selected regions or extend an image beyond its original borders.
- **ControlNet-style controls** — guide composition using poses, depth and edge references for precise structure.
- **Fine-tuned community models** — a catalogue of thirty-plus specialised models tuned for particular styles and subjects.
- **Image and video generation** — still-image creation alongside video, drawing on integrated third-party video models.
- **Custom model training** — train your own fine-tuned model on a dataset to lock in a consistent style.
- **API access** — programmatic generation for developers building Leonardo into their own products.

## Pros and Cons

**What works well**

Control is Leonardo's strongest suit. The real-time canvas, inpainting, outpainting and ControlNet-style guidance let you direct an image with a precision that prompt-only tools cannot match, which suits designers who know exactly what they want and need to iterate toward it. The catalogue of fine-tuned community models is the second advantage: rather than bending one general model to every task, you can pick a model already tuned for the style you are after, from illustration to product renders. Custom training extends that further for teams that need a repeatable house style. And the Phoenix model's improved prompt adherence and text rendering closed a gap that earlier versions struggled with.

**Where it falls short**

The abundance of controls is also a barrier. Leonardo can feel busy and technical to a newcomer, and the token-metered economy — where a daily allowance on the free tier and credit consumption on paid plans govern how much you can generate — takes some getting used to, especially during heavy sessions. Raw single-prompt aesthetics may not always match the most polished rivals; Leonardo's payoff comes from working an image rather than accepting the first result. There is also a strategic question hanging over any Canva-owned product: while Leonardo runs independently today, ownership by a larger suite always carries some uncertainty about long-term direction.

**A note on naming confusion**

Two mix-ups are worth clearing up. First, "Leonardo" as a product name collides with several unrelated tools, so specify leonardo.ai when searching or recommending it. Second, Leonardo does not have a feature called "Flow" — that is Google's AI filmmaking tool. The confusion arises because Leonardo integrates Google's Veo video models, not because it ships anything named Flow. Describing its real features avoids that trap.

## How to Use Leonardo AI

1. **Create a free account** — sign up at leonardo.ai and start with the daily token allowance to explore.
2. **Choose a model** — pick Phoenix, Kino XL, an integrated third-party model, or a fine-tuned community model suited to your style.
3. **Write and refine your prompt** — describe the subject and style, taking advantage of Phoenix's stronger prompt adherence.
4. **Open the real-time canvas** — move into the interactive surface when you want to shape composition rather than re-roll prompts.
5. **Use inpainting and outpainting** — repaint problem areas or extend the frame to adjust the image precisely.
6. **Apply ControlNet-style guidance** — supply pose, depth or edge references to lock down structure.
7. **Train a custom model** — if you need a consistent house style, train a model on your own dataset.
8. **Mind your tokens** — track consumption so a long session does not exhaust your allowance mid-project.

### FAQs

**Is Leonardo AI free?**
There is a free tier with a daily token allowance, which is enough to explore the canvas, models and editing tools before committing. Heavier or professional use pushes you toward the paid plans, where larger credit allowances and additional features live. It is a fair way to judge whether the control-first approach fits how you work.

**How much does Leonardo AI cost?**
Reported individual plans run roughly from around $12 a month up through higher tiers near $30 and $60, with team plans and API access alongside. The model is token- or credit-metered, so what you can produce depends on your allowance. These figures are second-hand and change, so verify the current pricing on the vendor page.

**Is Leonardo AI owned by Canva?**
Yes. Canva announced its acquisition of Leonardo.Ai in July 2024, in a deal reported above US$320 million, but Leonardo continues to operate as a standalone product and brand. Its Phoenix architecture also underpins Canva's own Dream Lab generator, which shows how closely the technology is now tied to its parent.

**Does Leonardo have a feature called Flow?**
No. Flow is Google's AI filmmaking tool, and the mix-up comes from Leonardo integrating Google's Veo video models. Leonardo's own capabilities include the real-time canvas, inpainting and outpainting, reference controls, fine-tuned models and custom training — but nothing it markets as Flow.
