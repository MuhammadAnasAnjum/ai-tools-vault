# Otter.ai Review: From Transcription Workhorse to Conversational Knowledge Engine

Most people meet Otter.ai the same way: someone drops a tidy, searchable transcript into a channel minutes after a call ends, and you find yourself wondering how they managed it. For years that was the whole pitch — reliable, real-time meeting transcription that turned rambling conversations into text you could actually search. In 2026 the product is reaching for something larger, and this review weighs both the dependable core and the ambitious new layer built on top of it.

The shift is deliberate. In April 2026 the company repositioned itself as a "Conversational Knowledge Engine" rather than a humble note-taker, and on 21 July 2026 it launched Live Assist, a feature meant to help during a meeting rather than only after it. That ambition deserves a fair hearing, but so does the privacy litigation now attached to the brand. An honest assessment has to hold the genuinely useful tooling in one hand and an unresolved consent debate in the other.

## What is Otter.ai?

Otter.ai is an AI meeting assistant. Its foundation is speech-to-text: it captures spoken conversation and produces a live, time-stamped transcript you can read, search, and share. Around that foundation it has layered summaries, action items, and an assistant you can question directly, so a recorded meeting becomes a source you can interrogate rather than a file you have to sit through again.

The headline agent is OtterPilot, which auto-joins calls on Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams so nobody has to remember to press record. Above transcription sit automated summaries and extracted action items, an AI Chat that answers questions across your meeting history, and a dedicated Sales Agent aimed at revenue teams. The April repositioning and the July Live Assist launch nudge the tool from passive record-keeping towards active, in-the-moment help. Recent additions also include MCP support, which lets Otter connect into the wider ecosystem of AI tools and agents.

## Key Features

- **Real-time transcription** — captures meetings as live, time-stamped text you can follow, search, and share while the conversation is still happening.
- **OtterPilot** — automatically joins Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams calls so recording never depends on someone remembering to start it.
- **AI Chat** — ask questions across your transcripts and get answers drawn from what was actually said, instead of scrolling through raw text.
- **Automated summaries and action items** — condenses a long call into key points and pulls out the tasks people committed to.
- **Live Assist** — launched on 21 July 2026 to offer help during a meeting, extending Otter from after-the-fact notes to in-the-moment support.
- **Sales Agent** — a purpose-built assistant for revenue teams that works over call content to support the sales workflow.
- **MCP support** — lets Otter plug into the broader landscape of AI agents and tools through the Model Context Protocol.
- **Cross-platform capture** — spans the major meeting platforms and syncs transcripts into one searchable central library.

## Pros and Cons

**What works well**

The core job is done well. Transcription is fast and accurate enough to trust for real work, and the automatic summaries and action items spare you the tedious write-up that usually lands on whoever is least busy. OtterPilot removing the "did anyone hit record?" scramble is a small thing that quietly reshapes team habits. The AI Chat is the standout of the newer additions: being able to ask "what did we decide about the budget?" and get an answer sourced from the actual conversation turns a pile of transcripts into something you can genuinely mine. For teams that live in back-to-back calls, the time recovered is real rather than theoretical.

**Where it falls short**

The serious caveat is legal, and it concerns consent. A class action, In re Otter.AI Privacy Litigation (No. 5:25-cv-06911, N.D. Cal.), was consolidated on 5 December 2025 and remains ongoing. It alleges that recording took place without the consent of all parties, with claims raised under the ECPA, CFAA, and CIPA. Precision matters here: these are allegations, and as of mid-2026 no court has ruled that Otter did anything unlawful. The case is unresolved. Yet the practical lesson holds however it ends — in many places you must have everyone's agreement before recording, and a tool that auto-joins calls makes that duty easy to overlook. Beyond the litigation, the swift repositioning carries the usual growing pains: features such as Live Assist are young, and the further Otter reaches into live assistance and sales work, the more it bumps against specialists that do one thing deeply.

**A note on consent before you deploy it**

None of this makes Otter a tool to avoid, but it does turn consent into a setup step rather than an afterthought. Tell participants they are being recorded, secure their agreement, and check the rules for your jurisdiction, particularly in all-party-consent regions. Handled openly, the privacy risk is manageable; ignored, it is the part of this product most likely to cause you trouble.

## How to Use Otter.ai

1. **Sign up and set your defaults** — create an account at otter.ai and start on the free tier to test transcription quality against your own meetings.
2. **Connect your calendar and platforms** — link Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams so OtterPilot can join scheduled calls automatically.
3. **Get consent first** — before recording, tell everyone on the call and confirm you have their agreement, especially where all-party consent is required.
4. **Let OtterPilot capture the meeting** — allow the assistant to join and transcribe in real time, or start a recording manually for in-person conversations.
5. **Review the summary and action items** — after the call, check the auto-generated summary and task list, correcting anything the model misheard.
6. **Query with AI Chat** — ask questions across your transcripts to surface decisions and details without re-reading everything.
7. **Share and follow up** — distribute the transcript or summary to attendees and assign the extracted actions.
8. **Explore the newer layers carefully** — try Live Assist or the Sales Agent where they fit, treating young features as helpful rather than the finished article.

### FAQs

**Is Otter.ai free?**
There is a free tier, reported to include around 300 minutes of transcription per month, which is enough to judge whether the tool suits you. Paid options are reported at roughly ~$16.99 per month for Pro and ~$30 per month for Business, with enterprise pricing available on request. Those figures are second-hand and plans shift, so confirm the current tiers and minute limits on the Otter site.

**What is the Otter.ai lawsuit about?**
A consolidated class action in the Northern District of California alleges that Otter recorded conversations without the consent of all participants, citing federal and California wiretap and computer-fraud statutes. It was consolidated in December 2025 and is ongoing. Crucially, no court has found that Otter acted unlawfully; the claims are unproven allegations at this stage.

**Do I need everyone's permission to record a meeting?**
Often, yes. Many jurisdictions, including several US states, require the consent of all parties before a conversation is recorded. Because OtterPilot can join automatically, the safe habit is to announce recording and get agreement every time, and to check your local rules.
