# Perplexity AI: The Answer Engine That Wants to Replace Your Search Bar

Most AI assistants are chat products that learned to search. Perplexity was built the other way around: search first, chat second. Ask a question and you get a written answer with numbered citations pointing at the sources behind each claim — closer to a research assistant's memo than a page of blue links.

That design makes Perplexity the strongest option for one specific job, and a mediocre choice for several others. Knowing which is which saves you a subscription.

## What is Perplexity?

Perplexity is an AI-powered answer engine. It searches the live web, reads what it finds, and synthesises a direct response with inline citations you can click to verify. The citations are the point — they turn an AI answer from something you have to trust into something you can check.

It runs its own **Sonar** model family alongside routed access to third-party frontier models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and others. Free users get automatic routing to whichever model the system judges best; choosing a specific model per query requires a paid plan.

The product has expanded well past a search box. **Comet**, its own browser, puts an AI assistant alongside every page you visit. **Spaces** collect research around a topic with your own uploaded files as sources. **Deep Research** produces long multi-source reports. And a **Computer** agent, rolled out to Pro subscribers in March 2026, carries out multi-step tasks on your behalf.

## Key Features

- **Cited answers by default** — every factual claim links to its source, so verification takes one click instead of a fresh search.
- **Deep Research** — spends several minutes reading many sources to produce a structured, referenced report on a topic.
- **Spaces** — persistent research workspaces that combine web results with documents you upload, useful for ongoing projects.
- **Comet browser** — available on desktop and, since March 2026, iOS, with the assistant available in-page rather than in a separate tab.
- **Model choice on paid plans** — route a query to a specific frontier model when you have a preference about how it will be handled.
- **400+ app connectors** with Model Context Protocol support, so answers can draw on your own tools and data.
- **Focused search modes** — restrict a query to academic papers, social discussion, or other source types instead of the open web.
- **Wide availability** — web, iOS, Android, macOS and Windows desktop, the Comet browser, and a separately billed API.

## Pros and Cons

**The case for it**

For research questions with a factual answer, Perplexity is faster and cleaner than either traditional search or a general chatbot. You skip the SEO-optimised filler and land on a synthesis with sources attached. Deep Research is legitimately strong for competitive scans, technical comparisons, and any question where you would otherwise open fifteen tabs. The free tier is usable rather than a teaser, and academic pricing at $10/month is good value for students.

**The case against it**

Accuracy has been the persistent criticism, and it is not a small one. Perplexity has been sued by Dow Jones and the New York Post, CNN, and the New York Times, with some claims specifically alleging that it attributed fabricated statements to real publishers. Hallucinated citations are the worst failure mode an answer engine can have, because the presence of a footnote implies verification that has not happened. Publishers have also criticised its handling of paywalled content. On the product side, Perplexity has stopped publishing numeric usage quotas for consumer plans, so what "limited" means on the free tier is genuinely unclear, and paid users have reported throttling in heavy weeks. It is also a weaker generalist than ChatGPT or Claude — for open-ended writing, brainstorming, or coding, it is not the tool you want.

## How to Use Perplexity

1. **Start at perplexity.ai** — no account is needed for basic searches, though signing in preserves your history.
2. **Ask a real question, not keywords.** "Which project management tools support dependency tracking under $15 per user?" works far better than "project management tools."
3. **Click the citations.** This is the habit that makes Perplexity trustworthy. Skim the linked sources on anything you plan to rely on or repeat.
4. **Ask follow-ups in the same thread.** It keeps context, so you can narrow and refine rather than starting over.
5. **Use a focus mode when precision matters.** Restricting to academic sources for a research question cuts out marketing content entirely.
6. **Reserve Deep Research for substantial questions.** It takes minutes rather than seconds, so use it where a real report is the goal.
7. **Build a Space for ongoing work.** Adding your own documents alongside web sources keeps a project's research in one place.

### FAQs

**How much does Perplexity Pro cost?**
Pro is $20/month or $200/year. A higher Max tier runs $200/month or $2,000/year and includes a monthly allowance of agent credits. Students can get Education Pro at $10/month, and Enterprise Pro is priced per seat. The Comet Plus add-on is $5/month standalone and is included with Pro and Max. Confirm figures on the official pricing page, since Perplexity revises tiers frequently.

**Can Perplexity replace Google?**
For questions with a factual answer, often yes — you get a synthesis instead of a results page. For navigational searches, local results, shopping, maps, and anything where you want to browse options yourself, Google remains better. Most people who adopt Perplexity end up using both rather than switching outright.

**Are Perplexity's citations always accurate?**
No, and this is the most important caveat in this review. The tool has faced litigation alleging that it attributed fabricated content to named publishers. Citations make verification easy, but they are not a guarantee that the source says what the summary claims. Click through before you quote, cite, or act on anything consequential.

**Is the free version good enough?**
For occasional research, yes. You get cited answers and limited access to the stronger search and research modes. Paid plans raise those limits, unlock manual model selection, and add the agent features. Use the free tier for a few weeks and upgrade only if you actually hit the ceiling.
