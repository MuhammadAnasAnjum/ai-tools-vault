# Semrush Review 2026: The All-in-One SEO Suite Adobe Just Bought

Semrush is one of the most established names in search marketing: a single platform that bundles keyword research, competitor analysis, site audits, rank tracking and content tools into one subscription. For more than a decade it has been the tool marketers reach for when they want to understand what rivals rank for, and why.

Two things make a 2026 review different from every older one. Semrush is now owned by Adobe, and it has pivoted hard towards tracking how brands appear inside AI answers. Both facts matter if you are weighing up a subscription today, so this review covers them plainly alongside the core toolkit.

## What is Semrush?

Semrush is an all-in-one SEO and competitive-intelligence suite. Rather than specialising in one job, it aims to cover the whole search-marketing workflow: finding keywords worth targeting, auditing a site for technical problems, tracking where you rank, analysing backlinks, and planning content. The appeal is consolidation, one login instead of five separate tools.

Its most distinctive strength has always been competitive data. You can enter a competitor's domain and see estimated traffic, their top-performing keywords, the pages driving their visibility and the backlinks pointing at them. That intelligence is where Semrush earned its reputation, and it remains the reason many agencies keep it.

In April 2026 the picture changed at the corporate level. Adobe completed its acquisition of Semrush, making it a wholly-owned subsidiary. Semrush is being folded into Adobe's customer-experience enterprise under the banner of brand visibility. For subscribers, the tool works as before today, but roadmap and packaging decisions now sit with Adobe, which is worth remembering on any annual commitment.

## Key Features

- **Keyword research** — a large keyword database with search volume, difficulty and intent, plus keyword-gap comparisons against competitors.
- **Competitor analysis** — estimated traffic, top pages and organic keyword profiles for any domain you enter.
- **Site audit** — an automated technical crawl that flags crawlability, speed and on-page issues with prioritised fixes.
- **Rank tracking** — daily position monitoring for your target keywords across locations and devices.
- **Backlink analysis** — a sizeable backlink index for auditing your own profile and researching others'.
- **Content marketing toolkit** — topic research, brief generation and an on-page SEO checker.
- **AI Visibility toolkit** — tracks whether your brand appears in AI answers from ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity and Gemini; sold separately.
- **Local and advertising tools** — listing management and paid-search research for broader marketing coverage.

## Pros and Cons

**What works well**

Depth and breadth are the headline strengths. Few tools match Semrush for the sheer amount of competitive data on tap, and for agencies juggling several clients the consolidation is genuinely valuable, with keyword research, audits, rank tracking and reporting all in one place. The competitive-intelligence side in particular is hard to replicate with cheaper point tools.

The new AI Visibility push is also timely. As more searches are answered inside AI tools rather than on a traditional results page, knowing whether your brand is mentioned there is becoming a real concern, and Semrush was early to build tracking for it. Generative Engine Optimization, measuring and improving presence in AI answers, was Adobe's stated reason for the acquisition.

**Where it falls short**

Cost is the recurring complaint. Semrush is expensive, and the pricing model compounds it: the plan you pick sets usage limits, and extra users are charged per seat, so a small team's bill climbs quickly. Pricing is reported around $140 a month for the entry Pro plan, roughly $250 for Guru and about $500 for Business, with Enterprise quoted individually and annual billing cheaper. Treat those as indicative and verify on the vendor page, as the checkout figures move.

The learning curve is steep. Semrush does so much that new users can feel lost, and getting value takes time and a willingness to learn the interface. The AI Visibility features, while promising, are new and evolving, priced as a separate add-on, and worth trialling rather than assuming they are mature.

**The Adobe acquisition, for context**

This is important enough to state clearly. Adobe signed a definitive agreement to buy Semrush in November 2025 and completed the deal on 28 April 2026. Semrush was previously listed on the New York Stock Exchange and is now delisted, operating as an Adobe subsidiary. Nothing about the tool breaks overnight, but Adobe has a history of integrating acquisitions into its enterprise suite, so expect packaging, pricing tiers and the roadmap to shift over the coming year. If you are signing an annual contract now, factor in that the Semrush you buy may be positioned differently by renewal.

## How to Use Semrush

1. **Start with a free account** — the limited free tier lets you run a handful of searches and get a feel for the interface before paying.
2. **Add your domain and set up a project** — this unlocks site audit, rank tracking and position monitoring for your own site.
3. **Run a site audit first** — fix the technical issues it surfaces before chasing new keywords, since a healthy site benefits more from everything else.
4. **Research competitors** — enter two or three rival domains to see the keywords and pages driving their traffic, then look for gaps you can target.
5. **Build a keyword list** — use the research tools to find terms with realistic difficulty and clear intent, and group them by topic.
6. **Track your rankings** — add your target keywords to position tracking so you can see progress over weeks, not days.
7. **Use the content toolkit** — generate briefs and check drafts against on-page recommendations before publishing.
8. **Trial AI Visibility separately** — if AI-answer presence matters to you, add the toolkit deliberately and measure whether it earns its extra cost.

### FAQs

**Is Semrush still independent after the Adobe acquisition?**
No. The deal completed on 28 April 2026 and Semrush is now a wholly-owned Adobe subsidiary, no longer publicly traded. The product still runs as before for now, but future decisions on pricing, packaging and roadmap sit with Adobe.

**How much does Semrush cost?**
Pricing is reported around $140 a month for Pro, roughly $250 for Guru and about $500 for Business, with Enterprise custom-quoted and annual billing cheaper. These figures are second-hand and change over time, so confirm the current rates on the vendor page before subscribing.

**What is Semrush AI Visibility?**
It is a toolkit that tracks whether and how your brand appears inside AI-generated answers from tools like ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity and Gemini, an area often called Generative Engine Optimization. It is priced separately from the core plans.

**Is Semrush worth it for a small business or beginner?**
It can be, but weigh the cost and learning curve honestly. Semrush is powerful and deep, which is exactly why it can overwhelm a solo user or small team. If you only need occasional keyword ideas, a lighter tool may suit better; if you need serious competitive intelligence, Semrush is hard to beat.
