# AdCopy Review: The Meta Ad Tool That Became Koast.ai

If you have been searching for "AdCopy" and feel confused, you are not alone. The tool that started life as AdCopy.ai has grown up, changed its name, and shifted its focus. This review clears up what it is today, what it does, and whether it belongs in your Meta advertising stack.

The short version: AdCopy.ai began in 2024 as an AI ad-copywriting tool and evolved into Koast.ai, a Meta ad-launching and automation platform. The actively developed product now lives under the Koast name, built by Reign Technologies in Tampa, Florida.

## What is AdCopy (now Koast.ai)?

Koast.ai is a platform for launching and automating advertising on Meta — that is, Facebook and Instagram only. Rather than writing a single ad, it is built for volume: bulk-publishing many ad variations across multiple ad accounts, generating ad copy with AI, and then managing those ads automatically once they are live.

Its automation is the selling point. Koast can pause underperformers with a "stop-loss" rule, adjust budgets automatically, produce AI performance reports and support team collaboration. The founders, Anthony Mastellone and Lucas Johnson, positioned it for media buyers and agencies who test heavily and need to move fast across accounts.

Put simply, the product answers a specific pain: launching and managing large volumes of Meta ads is repetitive, and doing it across several client accounts multiplies the tedium. Koast tries to compress that work into a single, faster workflow, where creative variations, copy and rules are handled together rather than in a dozen separate steps inside Ads Manager. That framing matters, because it tells you who will get value and who will not — this is a tool for people who launch a lot, not for someone running a single evergreen campaign.

One important clarification: there are three different things called "AdCopy". This one — AdCopy.ai, now Koast.ai, by Reign Technologies — is the Meta automation platform. A separate "AdCopy AI" by Tinuiti, powered by Gemini, launched in May 2026. There is also an unrelated tool on RapidAPI. And none of these is Copy.ai, the general copywriting app. Make sure you land on the right product before signing up.

## Key Features

- **Meta-only ad launching** — Publish Facebook and Instagram ads at scale from one place.
- **Bulk variation publishing** — Push many ad variations across multiple ad accounts at once.
- **AI ad-copy generation** — Draft headlines and primary text quickly for testing.
- **Stop-loss automation** — Automatically pause ads that underperform against your rules.
- **Budget automation** — Shift or adjust budgets without manual babysitting.
- **AI performance reports** — Summaries that surface what is working across campaigns.
- **Multi-account management** — Handle several ad accounts, useful for agencies.
- **Team collaboration** — Shared workspaces for media-buying teams.

## Pros and Cons

**What works well**

Koast is strong where it is focused. If your job is to launch fifty ad variations, kill the losers and scale the winners, the bulk publishing and stop-loss automation remove a great deal of manual work. Managing several ad accounts from one dashboard is a genuine benefit for agencies and freelancers who would otherwise switch between logins. The AI copy gives you a starting draft rather than a blank page, and because the whole product is built for Meta, the workflow feels purpose-made rather than bolted on.

**Where it falls short**

The narrow focus cuts both ways. Koast is Meta-only, so if you also run Google, TikTok, LinkedIn or YouTube campaigns, it will not help there and you will need other tools alongside it. It is also a young product from a fast-moving startup, which means features and pricing can shift and some areas are less mature than long-established competitors. The AI copy is a first draft, not a finished ad, so a human editing pass remains essential before anything goes live. It is best thought of as an accelerator for teams that already know how to run Meta ads, rather than a substitute for that knowledge.

**A note on billing and confidence**

Worth highlighting: we did not find a major billing controversy around Koast, which is reassuring given how common trial-related complaints are elsewhere in this category. That is a positive sign rather than a guarantee, so still review the current terms of the 5-day trial and confirm what happens at renewal before you enter card details. As with any young platform, keep an eye on your first invoice.

## How to Use AdCopy (Koast.ai)

1. **Confirm the right product** — Make sure you are signing up for Koast.ai by Reign, not another "AdCopy".
2. **Register and start the trial** — Create an account and begin the 5-day trial.
3. **Connect your Meta accounts** — Link the Facebook and Instagram ad accounts you manage.

5. **Build variations** — Assemble many ad variations ready for bulk publishing.
6. **Set automation rules** — Configure stop-loss and budget rules to manage ads automatically.
7. **Launch in bulk** — Publish your variations across accounts in one action.
8. **Review AI reports** — Check performance summaries and scale what works.

### FAQs

**Is AdCopy the same as Copy.ai?**
No. AdCopy (now Koast.ai) is a Meta ad-launching and automation platform by Reign Technologies. Copy.ai is a separate general-purpose copywriting tool. They are unrelated products.

**Which "AdCopy AI" is this review about?**
This is AdCopy.ai, now Koast.ai, by Reign Technologies. It is distinct from Tinuiti's Gemini-powered "AdCopy AI" launched in May 2026, and from an unrelated tool on RapidAPI.

**Does Koast work with Google or TikTok ads?**
No. Koast is Meta-only, covering Facebook and Instagram. For other networks you will need separate tools.

**How much does it cost?**
Pricing is reported to start around 79 dollars a month for a single account, with agency tiers roughly 149 to 199 dollars and custom enterprise pricing. There is a 5-day trial. Verify current pricing on the vendor page.
