# QuillBot: A Rewriting Tool, and the Rules You Need to Know Before Using It

QuillBot occupies an unusual position among AI writing tools. It is not trying to write for you — it reworks text you already have. That focus makes it excellent at a specific job and completely wrong for another, and the difference between those two is where students get into trouble.

This review covers both the product and the academic integrity question honestly, because you cannot sensibly evaluate one without the other.

## What is QuillBot?

QuillBot is an AI writing assistant built around paraphrasing. You paste text, choose a mode — standard, fluency, formal, academic, simple, creative, and several others, plus custom modes on Premium — and it rewrites the passage in that register.

Around that core sits a suite of related tools: a grammar and spelling checker, a summariser, a translator, a citation generator, an AI content detector, an AI Humanizer, and a plagiarism checker capped at 25,000 words per month on Premium.

It is owned by **Learneo, Inc.**, the parent company formerly known as Course Hero, whose stable also includes Scribbr, LanguageTool, Symbolab, CliffsNotes, and LitCharts. One point of clarification, since it surfaces in searches: Learneo lost a $75 million jury verdict to Post University in March 2026, but that case concerns Course Hero content and is unrelated to QuillBot.

The free tier caps the paraphraser at 125 words per run. Premium is $19.95 monthly or $99.95 annually, working out around $8.33 per month, with verified students able to take roughly 25% off through Student Beans, bringing it near $6.25 monthly. Small teams of two to ten seats are priced from about $7.50 each. There is no free trial, though a three-day money-back guarantee applies.

## Key Features

- **Paraphraser with nine preset modes** plus custom modes on Premium, giving real control over register and tone.
- **Grammar and spelling checker** that handles the mechanical corrections most writers miss in their own work.
- **Summariser** for condensing long sources into key points or a paragraph.
- **Citation generator** supporting the major academic styles.
- **Plagiarism checker** on Premium, capped at 25,000 words monthly.
- **AI content detector** — useful with caveats discussed below.
- **Translator** for cross-language drafting.
- **Wide integration** — web, Chrome extension, Microsoft Word add-in, macOS app, iOS, and Android.

## Pros and Cons

**What it does well**

For non-native English writers, QuillBot is one of the most practically useful tools available. Taking a sentence you have written and seeing it rendered fluently is a fast way to both fix the immediate text and learn the pattern. The mode system is the good design decision here — switching a paragraph between formal and simple registers gives you control that a general chatbot's "make this better" does not.

The Word and Chrome integrations mean it works where you already write rather than demanding you paste into another tab. Annual pricing is reasonable, and the student discount makes it genuinely cheap. As an editing layer over your own thinking, it earns its place.

**What to be careful about**

The free tier's 125-word limit is restrictive enough to be a demo rather than a working tool. The AI content detector is unreliable, particularly on mixed human and AI text, with false positives reported — do not treat its verdict as authoritative about anyone's writing, including your own.

QuillBot is also weak at original content. It reworks what exists; it will not develop an argument or generate substantive material from a brief. If that is what you need, a general assistant is the right tool.

**The academic integrity question, answered plainly**

This is genuinely contested and depends on your institution. Broadly: paraphrasing a source *and citing it properly* is legitimate academic practice, and using QuillBot to improve the fluency of your own writing is generally acceptable. Running an entire essay through it, or using the Humanizer to launder AI-generated text past a detector, is treated as misconduct at many institutions. A growing number of universities now name paraphrasing tools explicitly in their policies, and Turnitin flags paraphrased AI text at meaningful rates.

You will find statistics circulating about what percentage of universities ban these tools. Those figures trace back to marketing content for competing products rather than research, so ignore them. The only reliable answer is your own institution's policy — read it before you rely on any tool of this kind.

## How to Use QuillBot

1. **Read your institution's academic integrity policy first** if you are a student. This determines what is legitimate for you specifically, and it takes five minutes.
2. **Sign up at quillbot.com** and test the free paraphraser within its 125-word limit before paying.
3. **Write your own draft first.** QuillBot improves your thinking; it does not replace it. The tool works best on text that already says what you mean.
4. **Choose the mode deliberately** — academic for formal papers, fluency for smoothing non-native phrasing, simple for accessibility. The mode does most of the work.
5. **Paraphrase paragraph by paragraph, not wholesale.** You keep editorial control and catch places where the rewrite has altered your meaning.
6. **Always check the output against your intent.** Paraphrasers occasionally change technical meaning while improving readability, which is worse than an awkward sentence.
7. **Cite your sources regardless.** Rewording someone else's idea does not make it yours — this is the single most common misunderstanding about paraphrasing tools.

### FAQs

**Is using QuillBot considered plagiarism?**
Not inherently, but it can become plagiarism depending on how you use it. Paraphrasing a source without citing it is plagiarism whether a tool was involved or not. Improving your own writing's fluency is generally acceptable. Running whole essays through it, or using the Humanizer to disguise AI-generated work, is treated as misconduct at many institutions. Policies vary, so check yours rather than relying on general advice.

**How much does QuillBot Premium cost?**
Premium is $19.95 monthly or $99.95 annually, which works out to roughly $8.33 per month. Verified students can get approximately 25% off through Student Beans, bringing it near $6.25 monthly. Team plans start around $7.50 per user for two to ten seats. There is no free trial, but a three-day money-back guarantee applies.

**Is QuillBot's AI detector accurate?**
Not reliably, particularly on text mixing human and AI writing, where false positives have been reported. No AI detector on the market is dependable enough to accuse someone on its output alone. Treat any detector result, including QuillBot's, as weak evidence rather than proof.

**What is the free version limited to?**
The main constraint is a 125-word cap per paraphrasing run, along with restricted access to modes and the premium tools. It is enough to evaluate the quality but not enough for sustained work on longer documents.
