# tl;dv Review 2026: AI Meeting Notes, Fairly Assessed

Meeting notes are the chore nobody wants, and tl;dv is one of a wave of AI tools built to take the job off your hands. It joins your video calls, records them, transcribes what was said and produces a tidy summary, so you can stay present in the conversation instead of scribbling half-legible notes.

This tl;dv review looks at what the tool does across Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams, how its free and paid plans compare, and the recording-consent questions that come with any AI notetaker. It is a capable, security-conscious product, and its generous free tier makes it easy to try before you commit. The main responsibilities it hands back to you are around disclosure and budgeting for heavier AI use, both of which we cover in detail below.

## What is tl;dv?

tl;dv is an AI meeting recorder and notetaker that works across the three big video-conferencing platforms: Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams. It records calls, transcribes them and generates AI summaries, so a meeting becomes a searchable, shareable document rather than a fading memory. Its AI summarisation is powered through a partnership with Anthropic.

The tool is flexible about how it captures meetings. You can use a bot recorder — added via a Chrome extension — that joins the call as a participant, or a bot-free native desktop app that records without a visible bot in the room. That choice matters for teams that dislike an obvious recording bot appearing in every meeting.

Beyond capture, tl;dv leans towards revenue teams. It offers CRM synchronisation, multi-meeting reports that pull themes across several calls, and sales-coaching features that help managers review conversations. That makes it as much a sales-enablement tool as a personal notetaker, though individuals can happily use just the recording and summary basics.

## Pros and Cons

**What works well**

tl;dv's free tier is unusually generous: unlimited recordings and transcripts with no card required, which is rare in this category and makes it easy to trial in earnest. Integration breadth is another strength — supporting all three major meeting platforms plus CRM sync means it slots into most workflows without fuss. The bot-free desktop option is a thoughtful touch for teams who find recording bots intrusive, and the Anthropic-powered summaries are competent at turning long calls into actionable notes.

**Where it falls short**

The catch sits in the AI allowance. The free plan caps AI summaries at roughly ten a month, so anyone who relies on automatic recaps will quickly need a paid plan. Pricing is reported second-hand and varies with recurring "40% off annual" promotions — Pro is cited at around $29 per user monthly (nearer $18 billed annually) and Business around $98 (nearer $59 annually) — which makes headline figures slippery. It is also worth noting that some of the harshest critiques of tl;dv come from competitor blogs, so weigh those with a pinch of salt.

**Recording consent and privacy**

Any tool that auto-joins calls and records them raises legitimate consent questions, and this is a category-wide issue rather than a tl;dv scandal. Recording laws vary by jurisdiction — some require every party to consent — so the responsibility to disclose that a meeting is being recorded, and to get agreement, always rests with you.

To its credit, tl;dv addresses the security side seriously. It publishes assurances including SOC 2 Type II and GDPR compliance, states that customer data is not used to train AI, offers EU-based hosting, applies data anonymisation with its AI partner, and provides the bot-free desktop option for the privacy-conscious. In other words, the platform gives you the tools to be compliant; using them responsibly is your job.
