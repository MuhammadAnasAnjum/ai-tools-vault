# Runway Review 2026: Gen-4.5 and the State of AI Video

Runway has spent the last few years as one of the most talked-about names in generative video, and the 2026 version of the platform looks very different from the one many directory listings still describe. If you last checked in when Gen-2 or Gen-3 were the headline models, this Runway review will bring you up to date, because both have since been retired and the experience has moved on considerably.

The New York studio behind the tool, Runway AI, Inc., now positions itself squarely at the professional end of the market. That ambition brings genuinely impressive output alongside a steeper learning curve, a credit-based economy, and a legal backdrop worth understanding before you commit. Below we walk through what the platform does well, where it frustrates, and how to get a usable result on your first afternoon with it.

## What is Runway?

Runway is a browser-based creative suite for generating and editing video with artificial intelligence. Its flagship model as of late 2025 is Gen-4.5, a substantial upgrade that handles motion, prompt adherence and visual coherence far better than the earlier generations. Crucially, the older Gen-3 Alpha model was withdrawn during July 2026 and its faster Turbo variant shortly after, so any guide that still frames Runway around Gen-2 or Gen-3 is describing a product that no longer exists in that form.

Beyond raw generation, Runway bundles a growing toolkit of editing and performance features. It is used by independent filmmakers, marketing teams, music-video directors and agencies who want cinematic results without a full production pipeline. The company, sometimes styled RunwayML, has attracted significant investment, with a valuation reported in the region of $5.3 billion, which signals how seriously the industry is taking model-driven video.

## Key Features
- **Gen-4.5 generation** — The current text-to-video and image-to-video model, tuned for stronger motion and prompt accuracy.
- **Aleph 2.0** — A video-to-video editing layer that restyles or transforms existing footage rather than starting from scratch.
- **Act-One and Act-Two** — Performance-capture tools that drive facial animation and expression from a reference "driving" video.
- **Camera controls** — Directable virtual camera moves such as pans, zooms and orbits for a more deliberate shot language.
- **Motion brush** — Paint motion onto specific regions of a frame to guide where and how movement happens.
- **Multiple entry points** — Work from a text prompt, a still image or existing clips depending on the shot you need.
- **Credit-based rendering** — Generations draw from a credit balance that varies by plan and output settings.

## Pros and Cons

**What works well**
Runway's biggest strength is the quality ceiling. With Gen-4.5 and the surrounding tools, skilled users can produce short sequences that genuinely read as cinematic, and the editing features such as Aleph and the Act suite go well beyond simple text-to-video novelty. It is a coherent ecosystem rather than a single trick, and the browser-based delivery means there is no heavy local hardware requirement. For professionals who want directorial control, the camera and motion tools reward the effort you put in.

**Where it falls short**
That control comes at a price, both literal and figurative. The learning curve is real, and newcomers can burn through credits experimenting before they land a shot they like. The credit economy makes costs feel unpredictable, and the higher tiers are aimed at committed users rather than curious hobbyists. There is also a legal cloud: an artists' copyright class action is ongoing, and a separate class action filed in February 2026 alleges the company trained on YouTube videos, a claim that surfaced through reporting on an alleged internal dataset leak. None of this is settled, but it is context any professional buyer should weigh.

**A note on model versions**
Because Runway retires older models fairly aggressively, workflows built around a specific generation can shift under you. It is worth building with the current model and re-checking the changelog before relying on the platform for a deadline-driven project.

## How to Use Runway
1. **Create an account** — Sign up and start on the free allowance to get a feel for the interface before paying.
2. **Choose your model and mode** — Select the current Gen-4.5 model and decide between text-to-video, image-to-video or an editing tool.
3. **Write a clear prompt** — Describe the subject, action, setting and mood; specific prompts tend to produce more usable results.
4. **Add references** — Upload a starting image or driving video where the feature supports it for tighter control.
5. **Set camera and motion** — Use camera controls and the motion brush to direct movement rather than leaving it to chance.
6. **Generate and review** — Render a short clip, check it against your intent, and note how many credits it consumed.
7. **Iterate deliberately** — Adjust one variable at a time to conserve credits while you refine the shot.
8. **Export and finish** — Download your clip and take it into a conventional editor for grading, sound and assembly.

### FAQs
**Is Runway still using Gen-2 or Gen-3?**
No. Gen-4.5 is the flagship as of late 2025, and the Gen-3 Alpha and Turbo models were retired during July 2026. Treat older references as out of date.

**Does Runway have a free option?**
Yes, there is a free tier with a limited credit allowance, which is a sensible way to test the platform before subscribing.

**How much does Runway cost?**
Paid plans are tiered and credit-based, running from an entry Standard subscription up to a renamed top "Max" tier. The specific figures that circulate are second-hand, so confirm current pricing on the vendor page.

**Is Runway involved in any lawsuits?**
There is an ongoing artists' copyright class action, plus a February 2026 class action alleging training on YouTube content. These claims are unresolved at the time of writing.
